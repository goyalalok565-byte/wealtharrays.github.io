What this calculator does

Project how a one-time investment could grow over a chosen time horizon. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

A lump-sum projection uses compound growth: FV = P × (1 + r)^t. The calculator treats the return as a constant assumption for planning, not a guaranteed market outcome.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

For a lump-sum investment, separate the starting capital from the modeled gain and test at least three return assumptions. This makes it easier to see how much of the final number depends on the assumed rate rather than on the amount invested.

Inflation and taxes can materially change the purchasing power of the projected value. If the money has a known future purpose, compare the inflation-adjusted result with the amount you actually expect to need rather than focusing only on nominal growth.

Worked scenario

Enter ₹1,00,000, 10% and 10 years to create the baseline. Then change only the return to 8% and 12%. The projected outcomes move substantially even though the starting capital and time horizon stay fixed. That sensitivity is the useful lesson: a long-term lump-sum result is driven heavily by the assumed return, so a range is more informative than one precise-looking number.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What does a lump-sum projection assume?

It assumes one starting amount remains invested for the full period at the annual rate entered. It is a mathematical scenario, not a promise of that yearly return.

How sensitive is the result to the return rate?

Very sensitive over long periods. Compare lower, middle and higher assumptions to see how much the final value depends on the assumed rate.

Does the result include inflation?

The growth result is nominal unless an inflation input is explicitly used. A larger future balance can still have lower purchasing power.

What about taxes and investment fees?

They are not automatically deducted from a generic projection. Include product-specific costs and taxes separately when comparing actual outcomes.

Lump-sum example: ₹1,00,000 invested for 10 years

If ₹1,00,000 grows at an assumed 10% a year for 10 years, the mathematical projection is about ₹2,59,374 before fees, taxes and inflation. The original ₹1,00,000 is the capital; the remaining amount is modeled growth. Because a constant 10% return is only an assumption, compare lower and higher scenarios rather than treating ₹2.59 lakh as a forecast.

Formula and assumptions for this calculator

A lumpsum projection compounds one initial investment over the selected period: A = P × (1 + r)^t. The calculator also shows the gain over the original investment and estimated purchasing power after inflation.

How to validate your inputs

Use the published example inputs as a baseline: Investment amount: 100000; Expected annual return: 10; Investment period: 10; Expected annual inflation: 6. Confirm the unit, period and eligibility conditions for each input against your own documents or the relevant provider. Change one assumption at a time and compare the output with an independent statement, invoice or schedule before making a material decision.
