What this calculator does

Estimate EMI, interest and all-in cost for a phone, appliance or other financed purchase. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Financed amount = purchase price − down payment; EMI then follows standard amortizing-loan mathematics.

Inputs

The calculator uses purchase price, down payment, interest rate, term and processing fee. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Frequently asked questions

Is zero-interest financing really free?

Not necessarily; fees, bundled products or lost discounts can change the effective cost.

Why include processing fee?

Short loans can have a meaningful all-in cost from one-time fees.

Does this include late charges?

No.

Consumer-durable financing: compare the full purchase cost

Consumer-durable loans and point-of-sale instalment plans can make a purchase feel affordable by showing a monthly payment. The actual cost may also include processing fees, interest, taxes on fees, bundled insurance, lost cash discounts or penalties. Some offers are advertised as no-cost EMI while the discount structure or fees still affects the effective price. Use the calculator with the financed amount and rate disclosed in the agreement, not only the retail price.

Read the offer beyond the EMI banner

Check whether the lender finances the full price or only part of it, whether a down payment is required and whether the rate is flat or reducing balance. Confirm late-payment charges, foreclosure rules and whether missing a payment affects your credit record. A small monthly instalment can conceal a high effective annual cost if the term is long or fees are financed.

Borrow for a planned purchase

Compare the financed total with the cash price and avoid taking instalments that interfere with rent, food, utilities or emergency savings. For small purchases, fees can form a meaningful share of the loan. If a promotion's details are unclear, request the Key Facts Statement or equivalent disclosure and verify the total amount payable before accepting.

Compare financing with the true cash price

Before accepting a point-of-sale loan, ask for the cash price, financed amount, down payment, total instalments, processing fee, taxes on fees and any compulsory add-on. A promotion described as no-cost EMI may include a reduced cash discount or a fee that changes the effective cost. Check whether the quoted interest is flat or reducing balance and whether early closure is allowed without penalty. Confirm the payment dates and consequences of missed instalments, including possible credit-reporting effects. Compare the total amount payable with the amount you would pay in cash. If the instalment reduces money available for rent, utilities or emergency savings, reconsider the purchase or choose a lower-cost option.

Protect your payment history and cash flow

Before signing, confirm the monthly due date, grace period, late fee, bounce fee and whether missing one instalment can affect your credit report. Check if the agreement allows early repayment and whether an administrative fee applies. Do not rely on a salesperson's verbal description of a no-cost promotion; the final invoice and loan schedule should show the actual financed amount and charges. Make sure the instalment fits alongside existing loans and essential bills. If the purchase is not urgent, compare saving for it first with borrowing now. The calculator is best used before acceptance so you can compare alternatives while you still have a choice.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For a financed purchase of ₹60,000 over 12 months at an illustrative 15% annual reducing-balance rate, the EMI is about ₹5,417 and total payments are roughly ₹65,000 before fees. Compare that with paying cash and any cash discount, and include processing charges. If the offer says no-cost EMI, ask for the invoice price, discount, fees and repayment schedule in writing before comparing.
