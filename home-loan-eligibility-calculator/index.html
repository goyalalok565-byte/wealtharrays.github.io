What this calculator does

Use income, existing EMIs, an assumed FOIR, rate and tenure to estimate an indicative home-loan eligibility amount. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Eligible EMI capacity = monthly income × FOIR − existing EMIs; the supported loan is the principal that produces that EMI at the selected rate and term.

Inputs

The calculator uses monthly income, FOIR, existing EMIs, interest rate and loan term. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is FOIR?

It is a lender-style affordability ratio; actual thresholds differ by lender.

Is this an approval?

No. Credit, employment, property valuation and lender policy also affect eligibility.

Why subtract existing EMIs?

Existing obligations reduce monthly capacity for a new loan.

Home-loan eligibility is an estimate, not a sanction

Eligibility models usually estimate a possible loan amount from income, existing EMIs, a repayment term and an assumed interest rate. Lenders also assess credit history, employment, age, dependants, property value, documentation and their internal underwriting rules. The calculator's result is therefore a planning range, not a promise that a bank will approve that amount. Use take-home income and realistic recurring obligations rather than gross salary alone.

Understand the debt-to-income assumption

A higher permitted EMI ratio can produce a larger estimated loan, but it can also leave less money for food, school fees, medical costs, maintenance and savings. Test a more conservative ratio and include any credit-card, vehicle or personal-loan payments. If income varies, use a sustainable lower-month estimate instead of a best month.

What to prepare before applying

Review credit reports, salary slips or business income records, bank statements, existing loan schedules and property documents. Ask lenders how they treat bonuses, variable pay, co-applicant income, age at maturity and floating rates. Compare total interest and all charges in addition to the maximum eligible principal.

Why a lender may approve less than the estimate

A lender's assessment can differ because it may discount variable income, include existing credit obligations, apply an age-at-maturity limit or use a lower property valuation. Credit history, employment stability, documentation, dependants and the property itself can affect underwriting. Some lenders assess gross income while others apply deductions or internal obligations ratios; these policies are not universal. Before applying, reconcile salary slips or business income records with bank statements, list every EMI and review your credit report for errors. Ask for the lender's eligibility assumptions in writing. Use the calculator to understand a possible range, then plan a purchase around a conservative EMI that leaves room for essential spending and savings.

Set a personal borrowing ceiling

A lender's maximum and your own comfortable borrowing limit can be different. Build a monthly budget that includes essential household costs, insurance, maintenance, retirement savings and a contingency for repairs or income loss. Test the proposed EMI after increasing the interest rate and reducing discretionary income. If the purchase relies on future salary growth or a bonus, calculate affordability without that uncertain income. Consider the cash needed for the down payment and government charges before deciding how much to borrow. A conservative personal limit can reduce the risk of becoming house-rich but cash-poor even when the lender is willing to approve a larger amount.

Worked example with Indian rupees

Suppose a household takes home ₹1,20,000 per month and already pays ₹20,000 in EMIs. If a planning rule limits total monthly debt payments to 40% of take-home income, the illustrative total EMI ceiling is ₹48,000 and the remaining room is ₹28,000. This is only an affordability assumption; actual lender ratios vary. A lender may offer less after reviewing credit, expenses and the property.
