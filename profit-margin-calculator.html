Profit Margin

What this calculator does

The profit margin calculator shows profit as a percentage of revenue. It helps a business owner, student or analyst translate a profit amount into a margin and compare how changes in revenue or costs affect profitability.

Margin is a ratio, not the same thing as markup. Keeping those concepts separate prevents a common pricing mistake: adding a percentage to cost and assuming the resulting percentage is the same percentage of the final selling price.

How profit margin is calculated

The basic formula is Profit Margin = Profit ÷ Revenue × 100. If revenue is $100,000 and profit is $20,000, the margin is 20%. Gross margin, operating margin and net margin use different definitions of profit, so the meaning of the result depends on which profit figure is being used.

For a simple scenario, profit can be calculated as revenue minus the relevant costs. Real businesses may have multiple cost categories, taxes, financing costs and accounting treatments that require a more detailed income statement.

Worked example

Imagine a business has $50,000 of sales and $35,000 of costs included in the chosen profit definition. Profit is $15,000 and the margin is 30%. If revenue stays at $50,000 but costs rise to $40,000, profit falls to $10,000 and margin falls to 20%. The calculator makes that sensitivity easy to test.

Gross margin, operating margin and net margin

Gross margin generally focuses on revenue after the direct costs associated with producing or delivering goods or services. Operating margin goes further by considering operating expenses. Net margin is based on profit after the costs and items included in net income. A “good” margin therefore cannot be judged without knowing the industry, business model and accounting definition.

How to use the result responsibly

Use margin to understand profitability, but pair it with revenue growth, cash flow, debt, customer concentration and other relevant measures. A business can report a healthy accounting margin while still having cash-flow pressure.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays calculates the ratio from the values you enter. It does not interpret your accounts or determine which expenses belong in a particular accounting measure. For financial reporting, use your accounting records and applicable standards.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin measures profit as a percentage of selling revenue. Markup measures profit relative to cost. The same transaction can have different margin and markup percentages.

What is a good profit margin?

There is no universal target. Margins vary substantially by industry, business model, growth stage and accounting definition.

How can I improve margin?

Typical levers include pricing, product mix, direct costs, operating efficiency and waste reduction. The right action depends on why the margin is low.

Does the calculator include tax?

Only if your chosen profit figure already reflects tax or you include tax in the inputs. The calculator does not know your tax rules automatically.
