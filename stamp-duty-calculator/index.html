What this calculator does

Estimate stamp duty and registration costs on a property purchase using your local assumptions. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Stamp duty = property value × stamp-duty rate; registration = property value × registration rate.

Inputs

The calculator uses property value, stamp-duty rate and registration rate. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Frequently asked questions

Are stamp-duty rates universal?

No. Verify the current official rate for your location and transaction.

Is registration always a percentage?

Not everywhere; some jurisdictions use slabs or fixed charges.

Does this include brokerage?

No. Add brokerage, legal and inspection costs separately.

Stamp duty and registration: verify state-specific rules

Stamp duty and registration charges are transaction costs associated with property documents, but the rate and calculation base vary by jurisdiction and transaction. Some locations may have different rates or concessions based on buyer category, property type or ownership. A calculator can multiply a chosen rate by a chosen value; it cannot decide which legal rate applies to your agreement or whether a concession is available. Confirm current rules with the relevant state registration department.

Confirm both rate and valuation base

The rate is only half the calculation. The authority may compare the agreement value with a government-notified guideline or circle rate and apply the relevant base. Registration fees may be charged separately and can have minimums, caps or different rules. Ask the registrar or a qualified conveyancing professional which rule applies to the exact property and transaction date.

Keep transaction costs separate

Budget for stamp duty, registration, legal review, brokerage and other documented charges as separate line items. Do not assume that a builder's cost sheet includes every government fee or that a concession applies automatically. Save the official fee calculation and receipt with the property records. This tool is not a legal filing or an official fee quote.

Confirm the rule for the exact transaction date

Property duty can depend on the state or territory, document type, agreement value, government guideline value, ownership structure and any applicable concession. Rates can change through notifications, and the registration fee may be calculated separately with a cap or minimum. Do not assume a rate seen in another city or on an older website applies to your transaction. Before paying, check the relevant registration department's current calculator or schedule and retain the challan and receipt. Ask a conveyancing professional to review the agreement value and classification if the transaction is unusual. This tool multiplies the value and rate you enter; it does not provide a legal determination or official payment demand.

Budget registration and professional fees separately

The stamp-duty result may not include registration fees, document handling, legal review, brokerage or other transaction expenses. Some jurisdictions calculate fees using a notified valuation base and have minimums, caps or concessions that depend on the transaction. Ask for an official fee calculation before making payment and confirm the deadline and payment method. Keep the challan, receipt and registered document with the purchase records. If the agreement value differs from the government valuation or the property has an unusual ownership structure, seek clarification from the relevant registration office. This calculator is a budget estimate, not an official government calculator or legal opinion.

Worked example with Indian rupees

If a property agreement value is ₹80,00,000 and an illustrative duty rate is 6%, the simple estimate is ₹4,80,000 before registration fees or other charges. This is arithmetic only; 6% is not a universal rate. The legal calculation base may be agreement value, guideline/circle value or whichever rule applies under local law. Verify the rate and base before budgeting the final payment.
