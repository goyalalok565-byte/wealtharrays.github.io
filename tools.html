How to choose the right calculator

The right tool depends on the question you are trying to answer. For recurring investing, the SIP calculator focuses on regular contributions, while the lumpsum and compound-interest calculators focus on a starting amount. CAGR is useful when you want to express a start-to-end change as an equivalent annual rate, while ROI describes the total percentage change.

For borrowing, EMI, car-loan and personal-loan calculators focus on regular repayment mathematics. The debt-payoff calculator is useful when the question is how payment size or extra repayment changes a debt timeline. For planning, inflation and net-worth tools help put future purchasing power and your current financial position into context.

Use scenarios, not one perfect number

Financial calculators are most useful when you compare assumptions. A return, interest rate, inflation rate or working-hours assumption can materially change an output, especially over many years. Try a conservative case and a second case with a different assumption, then identify which input has the biggest effect.

The calculator gives you the arithmetic implied by your inputs. It does not know the complete terms of your bank account, investment product, employment contract or tax situation. Check current fees, taxes, eligibility rules and official documentation before making a material financial decision.

What the Wealth Arrays calculators are designed to do

Our tools are browser-first educational utilities. They are designed to make common formulas easier to understand, provide transparent assumptions and let you test scenarios without creating an account. Results are estimates for education and planning, not personalised financial, tax or legal advice.

HOW TO USE THE LIBRARY

Choose the calculation that matches your question

The Wealth Arrays calculator library is organised around practical financial questions. Start with the tool whose inputs match the situation you are trying to model, then read the explanation below the calculator to understand the formula, assumptions and limitations.

Investment and growth

Use SIP, compound-interest, CAGR, ROI and related tools to explore how contributions, starting amounts, rates and time interact. Investment results are scenarios, not guarantees. If the calculation assumes a constant annual return, remember that real returns can vary from period to period.

Loans and borrowing

EMI, mortgage and debt tools can help you understand payment mathematics and the effect of tenure or interest rates. Compare the result with the lender's actual statement or offer. Fees, variable rates, payment timing and contractual rules can change the final cost.

Income, tax and planning

Salary, tax, retirement and related calculators help translate everyday assumptions into numbers you can compare. Tax rules and thresholds can change by jurisdiction and tax year, so verify current official guidance before using a result for a filing or payment decision.

A better way to compare scenarios

Write down your inputs, run a baseline, then change one important assumption. This makes it easier to see what is driving the result. If a small assumption change creates a large output change, treat that sensitivity as useful information rather than choosing whichever scenario produces the most attractive number.
