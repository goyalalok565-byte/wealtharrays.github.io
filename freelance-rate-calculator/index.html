What this calculator does

Estimate a target billable hourly rate from income goals, expenses and available billable time. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

Target rate = (desired annual income + annual business expenses) ÷ annual billable hours. Taxes, unpaid leave and profit buffers should be considered separately.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

Billable utilization is the critical variable for freelancers. If a target requires 1,200 billable hours at ₹1,167/hour but your realistic capacity falls to 960 hours, the required rate rises materially even before adding a tax buffer.

Build unpaid leave, sales, proposals, administration, learning time and gaps between projects into your workload assumptions. Then add a separate tax reserve and compare the resulting rate with project scope and client value rather than treating the hourly figure as a universal market price.

Worked scenario

Use ₹12,00,000 target income, ₹2,00,000 annual expenses, 25 billable hours per week and 48 working weeks. The calculator divides ₹14,00,000 by 1,200 billable hours to get about ₹1,167/hour. Then test 20 billable hours per week: the required rate rises because the same annual target has to be earned from fewer client hours. Add a separate tax buffer when setting your quote, and account for unpaid leave, sales, admin and gaps between projects.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Why calculate a billable hourly rate?

Not every working hour is billable. Sales, proposals, admin, revisions, learning, holidays and project gaps consume time, so a rate based on all hours can underprice your services.

What billable utilization should I assume?

There is no universal percentage. Start with actual billable hours, then test a lower-utilization case to create a buffer for slow periods and non-client work.

Should the rate include taxes and business costs?

Account for business expenses and a realistic tax buffer if your goal is a specific take-home amount. Adjust the inputs to match what your target income means to you.

Why can a higher rate still produce lower annual income?

If billable hours fall sharply, a higher rate may not compensate for low utilization. Compare rate and realistic billable capacity together.

Freelance-rate example: ₹12 lakh target with ₹2 lakh expenses

Suppose your target annual income is ₹12,00,000, business expenses are ₹2,00,000, and you expect 25 billable hours a week for 48 weeks. That is 1,200 billable hours a year, so the baseline target rate is about ₹1,167 per billable hour before personal taxes. The rate must cover non-billable work and business overhead, not just client delivery hours.

Formula and assumptions for this calculator

A target freelance hourly rate starts with the annual income you want to earn, adds annual business expenses, and divides by realistic billable hours: hourly rate = (target income + expenses) ÷ billable hours.

How to validate your inputs

Use the published example inputs as a baseline: Target annual income: 1200000; Annual business expenses: 200000; Billable hours per week: 25; Working weeks per year: 48. Confirm the unit, period and eligibility conditions for each input against your own documents or the relevant provider. Change one assumption at a time and compare the output with an independent statement, invoice or schedule before making a material decision.
