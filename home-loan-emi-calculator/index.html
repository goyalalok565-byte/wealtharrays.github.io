What this calculator does

Estimate the monthly payment and long-run cost of a home loan using a fixed interest rate and repayment term. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

EMI = [P × r × (1+r)^n] ÷ [(1+r)^n − 1].

Inputs

The calculator uses Home loan amount, Annual interest rate, Loan term. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include property taxes and insurance?

No. It models the loan itself; recurring housing costs should be considered separately.

Why is total interest high on a long loan?

A longer term leaves the balance outstanding for more months, so interest accumulates for longer.

Can I use a variable-rate loan?

The model assumes the entered rate stays constant; recalculate when the applicable rate changes.

Home loan EMI: payment, interest and affordability

A home-loan EMI is the fixed monthly instalment used to repay principal and interest on a loan at a stated rate and term. The estimate is useful before comparing offers because it separates the monthly commitment from the total amount repaid. It does not include stamp duty, registration, maintenance, property tax, insurance, processing fees or rate-reset effects unless those are separately entered. A lender may calculate eligibility from income, existing obligations, credit history, property valuation and its own policy; an EMI result alone does not establish approval.

What changes a home-loan EMI?

Principal, interest rate and tenure are the main drivers. Increasing the loan amount increases the payment proportionally; a higher rate raises both the EMI and total interest. Extending tenure usually reduces the monthly EMI but keeps the balance outstanding for longer. For floating-rate loans, an unchanged EMI may not be guaranteed: a lender can change the instalment, remaining term or both after a rate reset. Keep the assumptions aligned with the sanction letter when comparing offers.

Budget beyond the instalment

A responsible housing budget includes the down payment and transaction costs as well as recurring ownership costs. Keep a cash buffer after paying the down payment, and stress-test the budget against higher rates, repairs and income disruption. Compare APR or total borrowing cost, processing charges, prepayment terms and rate-reset rules—not only the advertised EMI. If the home is jointly financed, model who pays which share and whether the household can still cover essential expenses if one income temporarily stops.

A pre-purchase affordability checklist

Before applying, list the down payment, stamp duty, registration, moving and furnishing costs separately from the loan principal. Keep an emergency reserve after the purchase rather than using every available rupee at closing. Ask the lender whether the rate is fixed, floating or fixed only for an initial period, and how rate changes affect the EMI or tenure. Confirm whether insurance is optional or compulsory and whether processing charges are paid upfront or financed. For a joint application, test the household budget on one income as well as both incomes. The calculator estimates repayment under entered assumptions; it does not validate title, valuation, approval or the affordability of the whole purchase.

Compare the lender's written offer

For each lender, record the sanctioned principal, rate type, reset frequency, tenure, EMI, total repayment, processing fee and prepayment conditions in one table. Ask whether the lender's quoted rate is linked to a benchmark and what spread applies. Confirm whether the first instalment date or any pre-EMI interest changes the cash flow. If you are comparing a longer and shorter tenure, calculate the difference in total interest and consider whether you can make voluntary prepayments without penalty. Recheck the estimate if the final loan amount changes after the property valuation or down-payment adjustment.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For a ₹40,00,000 loan at 8.5% a year over 20 years, the standard monthly reducing-balance formula gives an EMI of about ₹34,700. Total payments over 240 months are roughly ₹83.3 lakh, so interest is about ₹43.3 lakh before fees. Treat this as an illustration: the exact result depends on rate, payment timing and rounding. Compare it with 7.5% and 9.5%, and test a shorter term to see the trade-off between monthly affordability and lifetime interest.
