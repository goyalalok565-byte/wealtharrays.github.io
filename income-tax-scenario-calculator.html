What this calculator does

This tool models a simple tax scenario: gross income minus deductions produces a planning income base, and that amount is multiplied by the effective tax rate you enter. It is deliberately transparent rather than pretending to know every country's current tax rules.

The result is useful for sensitivity testing. Build a conservative, middle and high-tax scenario and compare how much income remains after the assumed tax. That is different from calculating a legally accurate tax liability.

Why this is not official tax software

Tax systems can include progressive brackets, rebates, credits, exemptions, allowances, deductions, residency rules, filing status, special treatment of different income types and frequent rule changes. A generic percentage model cannot reproduce those details reliably across jurisdictions.

For that reason, Wealth Arrays does not describe this tool as an official tax calculator. Verify current rules with the relevant tax authority or an appropriately qualified professional before filing or making a compliance decision.

How to use it responsibly

  1. Enter a realistic income figure for the scenario you want to test.
  2. Enter only deductions that belong in the simplified model you are testing.
  3. Use the effective tax rate as an assumption, not as a claim about a specific country's current tax table.
  4. Compare multiple scenarios rather than treating one output as a guaranteed bill.

Methodology and limitations

The calculation is deterministic: identical inputs produce identical outputs. It does not fetch live tax rates, identify your jurisdiction, determine filing status, apply current brackets or verify eligibility for a deduction or credit. Currency selection changes monetary display only; it is not foreign-exchange conversion.

See the calculator methodology, disclaimer and contact page for correction reporting and limitations.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official tax calculator?

No. It is a jurisdiction-neutral scenario model using the effective tax rate you enter.

Why can my actual tax bill differ?

Actual tax can depend on brackets, deductions, credits, allowances, residency, filing status, income types and current local rules.

Should I use this for filing taxes?

No. Use current official tax guidance or qualified professional advice for filing and compliance decisions.
