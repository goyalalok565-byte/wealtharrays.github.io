What this calculator does

Estimate a gratuity amount using the common 15/26 salary formula, subject to eligibility and applicable rules. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

For an employee covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, the common estimate is last-drawn monthly basic salary plus dearness allowance × 15 ÷ 26 × completed years of service. Eligibility and exceptions depend on the applicable law and employment circumstances.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Last-drawn monthly basic + DA; Completed years of service; Estimated gratuity (0 if threshold not met); General 5-year threshold met (1 = yes). Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Last-drawn monthly basic + DA: 50000; Completed years of service: 10. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Is five years of service always required?

Five years of continuous service is the general rule for many cases under the Act, but exceptions and legal interpretation can apply, including certain death or disablement cases.

Does this include every component of salary?

No. The common formula uses last-drawn basic salary plus dearness allowance, not necessarily total CTC or every allowance.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

Gratuity estimate: salary basis and service eligibility

A gratuity estimate commonly uses the last-drawn basic salary plus dearness allowance, completed years of service and the applicable formula. Under the common formula for covered employees, the amount is salary × 15 ÷ 26 × eligible years. Eligibility, rounding of service, statutory caps and exceptions depend on the law and employment circumstances. This calculator provides a planning estimate and does not determine whether a specific employee is legally entitled to a payment.

Use the correct salary figure

The common statutory formula does not automatically use total cost to company or every salary component. Confirm which components qualify as wages for the relevant employment and legal context. Use completed service years as required by the applicable rules and do not assume that rounding conventions are identical for every case.

Check eligibility and applicable limits

Five years of continuous service is a general rule in many covered cases, but legal exceptions can apply, including certain death or disablement situations. Statutory ceilings and amendments may affect the payable amount. This calculator cannot verify service records or current legal interpretation. Consult official labour guidance or a qualified professional for a disputed or high-value claim.

Worked example with Indian rupees

With last-drawn monthly basic plus DA of ₹50,000 and 10 completed years, the common formula gives ₹50,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 10 = about ₹2,88,461.54 before considering any applicable statutory ceiling or special circumstances. The estimate uses the salary definition entered, not total CTC or every allowance. Verify the applicable Act, service record and employer calculation before relying on a final settlement figure.
