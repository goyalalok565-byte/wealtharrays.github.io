What this calculator does

Estimate an illustrative gratuity amount using a common 15/26 wage formula and general five-year threshold; fixed-term and statutory exceptions are not assessed. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

For many covered employees, a common illustration is eligible monthly wages × 15 ÷ 26 × completed years of service. The Code on Social Security, 2020 took effect on 21 November 2025; statutory wage definitions and transition treatment for earlier service can affect the result. This calculator applies only a general five-year threshold and does not assess fixed-term eligibility, every exception, statutory caps or an individual's legal entitlement.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Monthly eligible wage base (illustrative); Completed years of service; Illustrative gratuity amount (general 5-year rule; exceptions not assessed); General 5-year threshold met (not a legal eligibility decision). Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Monthly eligible wage base (illustrative): 50000; Completed years of service: 10. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does this calculator determine gratuity eligibility?

No. It applies a general five-year threshold for illustration and does not assess fixed-term employment provisions or other statutory exceptions under the Code on Social Security, 2020. Check current Ministry guidance and your employment facts.

What wage figure should I enter?

The statutory wage definition may differ from basic pay plus dearness allowance or total CTC. This tool accepts one simplified monthly wage-base estimate; verify the applicable components and transition rules with current Ministry guidance or a qualified professional.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

Gratuity estimate: salary basis and service eligibility

An indicative gratuity estimate commonly uses the last-drawn eligible wage base, 15 days' wages per completed year of service and the applicable service period. India's Code on Social Security, 2020 took effect on 21 November 2025; the statutory wage definition and transition treatment for service before that date can affect the calculation. This calculator's salary input is a simplified proxy and does not model every legal definition, eligibility rule, fixed-term employment provision, cap or transition. It is a planning estimate, not an entitlement decision.

Use the correct wage base

The statutory definition of wages is not necessarily the same as basic pay plus dearness allowance or total cost to company. The Code on Social Security, 2020 contains a defined wage calculation and the Ministry's current FAQs explain which components count. This calculator's single salary input cannot classify every allowance or remuneration component; verify the wage base applicable to the employment.

Check eligibility and transition rules

The Code on Social Security, 2020 took effect on 21 November 2025. The Ministry's FAQs describe how service before that date is treated, the general continuous-service threshold and specific fixed-term employment provisions. Eligibility, service calculation, statutory limits and exceptions depend on the employee's circumstances. Do not treat this estimate as a legal entitlement or final employer computation.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For illustration only, if the applicable monthly wage base were ₹50,000 and 10 eligible years were used, the common 15/26 calculation gives ₹50,000 × 15 ÷ 26 × 10 = about ₹2,88,461.54. This example does not determine which wage components, service-period rules or transition provisions apply to an individual employee. Verify the current law and employer calculation before relying on a final settlement figure.
