What this calculator does

Estimate how long a fixed monthly payment could take to clear one debt balance. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

The payoff calculation solves the amortization equation for time. If the payment does not exceed monthly interest, the balance will not amortize under a fixed-payment assumption.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

The first useful check is whether the monthly payment is comfortably above monthly interest. At ₹1,00,000 and 18%, the first month's interest is about ₹1,500; a ₹3,000 payment therefore initially reduces principal by about ₹1,500.

Use a second scenario with a higher payment to quantify the benefit of accelerating repayment. Keep new purchases, fees and rate changes outside the simplified model unless the calculator explicitly supports them, and reconcile the final payoff amount with the creditor.

Worked scenario

Use ₹1,00,000 balance, 18% annual interest and a ₹3,000 monthly payment. Then run a second scenario at ₹4,000 per month without changing the balance or rate. The comparison shows the value of directing an extra ₹1,000 toward the debt. If the payment is at or below the monthly interest, the balance will not amortize normally, so check that condition before relying on a payoff date.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the first payment include so much interest?

Interest is calculated on the outstanding balance. With a high rate, a meaningful part of the first payment can go to interest before the rest reduces principal.

What if my payment is less than monthly interest?

The balance may not fall and can grow under some debt terms. If payment does not cover required interest and charges, contact the creditor.

How much can a higher payment change payoff time?

Even a moderate increase can reduce months and future interest because principal falls faster. Compare the current payment with a realistic higher payment.

Should I trust the payoff date?

Use it as a planning estimate. Lender-specific fees, daily interest, payment timing and rate changes can alter the actual payoff.

Debt-payoff example: ₹1 lakh balance at 18%

Suppose you owe ₹1,00,000 at 18% annual interest and can pay ₹3,000 each month. The first month's interest alone is about ₹1,500, so only about ₹1,500 initially reduces principal. The calculator helps show why increasing the payment can shorten the payoff period and reduce total interest.
