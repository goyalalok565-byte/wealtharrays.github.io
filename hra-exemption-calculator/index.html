What this calculator does

Estimate the HRA exemption under the common salary-based method using editable salary, rent and city assumptions. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

The estimated exemption is the lowest of actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of basic salary plus DA, and the basic salary plus DA multiplied by the city percentage you enter (commonly 50% for specified metro cities and 40% elsewhere). Negative values are treated as zero. Tax rules and eligibility can vary.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Monthly basic salary + DA; Monthly HRA received; Monthly rent paid; City percentage (50 or 40); Estimated HRA exemption per month; Estimated taxable HRA per month; Annual estimated exemption. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Monthly basic salary + DA: 50000; Monthly HRA received: 20000; Monthly rent paid: 25000; City percentage (50 or 40): 50. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Which city percentage should I enter?

The common calculation uses 50% for specified metro cities and 40% for other locations. Check the current tax rules for your circumstances.

Does this decide my final tax liability?

No. It estimates one HRA exemption component and does not calculate your complete tax liability or confirm eligibility for a tax regime.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

HRA exemption: apply the lowest-of-three method carefully

The common HRA exemption calculation compares actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of eligible salary, and a salary-based percentage that depends on the applicable city category. The lowest eligible amount is used, subject to the tax rules and conditions for the relevant year and regime. This tool estimates one component; it does not calculate the full income-tax liability, validate rent receipts or determine final eligibility for a tax regime.

Rent, salary and city percentage

The salary base and rent amount must match the applicable tax rules and period. The commonly used 50% percentage applies to specified metro cities; 40% is commonly used elsewhere, but verify the relevant law and tax year. Do not enter annual rent alongside monthly salary or mix gross rent with the amount actually paid for the period.

Keep evidence and check the tax regime

Rent receipts, payment records, landlord details and any required declarations may be relevant to an employer or tax filing. Eligibility for HRA treatment depends on the applicable regime and current rules; the calculator does not review documents or determine the complete tax outcome. Confirm current Income Tax Department guidance or consult a tax professional before filing.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For an illustration, assume monthly basic salary plus DA of ₹50,000, HRA received of ₹20,000, rent paid of ₹25,000 and a 50% city percentage. The three amounts are ₹20,000, ₹20,000 (rent minus 10% of salary), and ₹25,000. The estimated monthly exemption is the lowest: ₹20,000. If rent were ₹18,000, the rent-based limit would be ₹13,000 and would reduce the estimate. Verify the correct salary and city inputs.
