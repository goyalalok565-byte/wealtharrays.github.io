What this calculator does

Estimate the doubling time with the Rule of 72, compare it with exact compounding and see the time to reach any growth multiple. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual rate in percent. Exact compounding: years to double = ln(2) ÷ ln(1 + rate). Years to reach a multiple M = ln(M) ÷ ln(1 + rate). The rule is a mental shortcut that is most accurate for rates between roughly 6% and 10%.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Annual return or inflation rate; Target growth multiple; Years to double (Rule of 72); Years to double (exact compounding); Rule of 72 minus exact; Years to reach the target multiple. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

Frequently asked questions

Why 72 and not 70 or 69?

72 divides evenly by many common rates such as 6, 8, 9 and 12, which makes mental arithmetic easy. 69.3 is more exact for continuous compounding, and 70 is often used for low rates.

Is the rule accurate at very high or very low rates?

It drifts. At very low rates the shortcut slightly overstates the doubling time and at very high rates it understates it. The exact figure on this page shows the size of the gap.

Does it work for inflation?

Yes. Dividing 72 by the inflation rate gives the approximate years in which prices double, which is also the time purchasing power halves.

When the Rule of 72 is good enough and when it is not

The Rule of 72 is a quick way to turn a percentage into a time. Divide 72 by the annual rate and you get the approximate number of years for money to double. It works for returns and also for inflation, where it tells you when prices double and when purchasing power halves. The shortcut is closest to the exact answer for rates in the middle range of about 6% to 10%. This calculator shows both figures and the difference so you can see when the shortcut starts to drift. It also converts any target multiple, such as three times or ten times, into years using exact compounding.

Use it for inflation as well as returns

If inflation runs at 6% a year, prices roughly double every 12 years. That means a fixed amount of cash loses half of its buying power over the same period. Entering the inflation rate here shows how quickly purchasing power erodes, which is a useful way to judge whether a savings rate that does not beat inflation is really growing your wealth.

Why the exact figure can differ from the shortcut

The Rule of 72 is an approximation of a logarithm. It is more accurate near 8% and gradually drifts at very low and very high rates. For decisions that involve large sums, such as a long-term goal, rely on the exact compounding figure shown on this page or on a full growth projection. Remember that a stated rate is an assumption and real returns vary from year to year.

Worked example with Indian rupees

At an 8% annual return the Rule of 72 gives 9 years to double and exact compounding gives about 9.01 years, so the shortcut is off by only about 0.01 years. To triple the money at 8%, the exact time is about 14.27 years. At 6% the rule gives 12 years against an exact 11.9 years, and ten times the money takes about 39.5 years. At 12% the rule gives 6 years while the exact figure is about 6.12 years.
