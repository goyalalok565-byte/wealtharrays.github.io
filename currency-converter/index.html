What this calculator does

Convert between major currencies with a transparent user-entered exchange rate. It does not claim to provide live FX rates. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Converted amount = source amount × exchange rate.

Inputs

The calculator uses From currency, To currency, Amount, Exchange rate (1 from = target). Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

Frequently asked questions

No. The rate is supplied by you so the calculation remains transparent.

Use a current rate from your bank, broker or another reputable FX source for material transactions.

Currency conversion with a rate you can verify

This converter uses the exchange rate you enter; it does not fetch live foreign-exchange quotes. That makes it suitable for checking arithmetic, comparing a quoted rate or estimating a budget when you already have a rate from a bank, card network or reputable market source. The result may differ from the amount ultimately received because providers can add a spread, transfer fee, cash-handling charge or card foreign-transaction fee. Always note the rate source and the time it was quoted.

Check the direction and units

A quote such as 83 INR per USD means one US dollar costs ₹83. To convert rupees to dollars, divide the rupee amount by 83; to convert dollars to rupees, multiply dollars by 83. A quote expressed in the inverse direction must be inverted before use. Never compare two quotes until both use the same base and quote currency and refer to the same transaction direction.

Why your final amount may differ

Retail conversion rates are not identical to mid-market reference rates. Banks, exchange counters, remittance services and card issuers may use different spreads and fee schedules. For travel, compare the final amount after fees; for international transfers, include intermediary and recipient-bank charges where relevant. This tool is a calculator, not a live quote service, and should not be presented as guaranteeing the rate available at checkout.

A practical way to compare provider quotes

When comparing two exchange providers, record the amount you send, the exchange rate, every fee, the amount the recipient receives and the timestamp of the quote. Compare the final delivered amount rather than the headline rate alone. For card purchases, the network rate, issuer markup and transaction fee can differ from a cash exchange quote. For remittances, intermediary banks or recipient charges may reduce the final amount. Keep the quote direction consistent: INR per USD and USD per INR are reciprocals, not interchangeable values. If a provider updates its rate before settlement, recalculate with the final quoted rate. This page performs arithmetic only and does not fetch or guarantee current market rates.

Keep a record of the rate source

For a business invoice, travel budget or transfer, write down the source of the rate, the quote time and whether it is a buy or sell rate. A bank may offer different rates for cash, card spending and remittance. A market reference rate can be useful for comparison but is not necessarily available to a retail customer. If the transaction settles later, compare the final debit and credit amounts with the quote and fee schedule. Avoid using this tool as a substitute for a provider's checkout quote, and never interpret the converted amount as a guaranteed payout.

Worked example with Indian rupees

If you convert ₹10,000 at an illustrative rate of ₹83.00 per US dollar, the arithmetic result is about US$120.48 before fees (₹10,000 ÷ 83). If a provider offers ₹81.50 per dollar instead, the same amount buys about US$122.70 only if the direction and quoted rate convention are interpreted correctly—so check whether the quote is INR per USD or USD per INR. Use the actual provider quote and calculate fees separately.
