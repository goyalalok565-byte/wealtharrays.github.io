What this calculator does

Calculate a simple snapshot of assets, liabilities and estimated net worth. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

Net worth is straightforward accounting: total assets minus total liabilities. The usefulness comes from defining inputs consistently and updating the snapshot over time.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

Net worth is a balance-sheet snapshot, so use consistent valuation dates and categories. Keep liquid cash, investments, property and liabilities visible rather than allowing one rising asset value to hide increasing debt or weak liquidity.

Repeat the calculation over time to identify the direction of change. Avoid double-counting a property-related loan or an investment account, and distinguish net worth from emergency liquidity: a ₹30 lakh home is an asset but is not the same as ₹30 lakh available to spend.

Worked scenario

Enter ₹2,00,000 cash, ₹5,00,000 investments, ₹30,00,000 property and ₹15,00,000 liabilities. The calculator produces ₹37,00,000 of assets and ₹22,00,000 net worth. To make the snapshot useful, repeat it monthly or quarterly using the same categories. A rising property value can increase net worth while liquid cash stays unchanged, so track liquidity and debt separately.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What should I include in net worth?

List assets such as cash, investments and property, then subtract debts and other liabilities. Use consistent categories and avoid double counting.

How should I value my home?

Use a reasonable current value if the purpose is present net worth, and record the method consistently. Market value does not mean the asset is immediately liquid.

Why is net worth different from available cash?

Net worth includes assets that may be difficult or costly to sell. Someone can have high net worth but limited liquid cash.

How often should I track net worth?

Monthly or quarterly tracking can reveal trends without excessive daily noise. Use the same valuation method each time so changes are comparable.
