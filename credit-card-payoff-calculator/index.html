What this calculator does

Model how a credit-card balance changes when you pay a fixed amount each month at an assumed APR. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

Each month, interest is added using the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12), then the fixed payment is deducted. The estimate excludes fees, new purchases, changing APRs and minimum-payment rules. If the payment does not exceed the first month's interest, the balance will not decline.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Current card balance; Annual percentage rate (APR); Fixed monthly payment; Months to clear (0 = payment too low); Monthly interest at current balance; Payment needed to start reducing balance; Months to pay off; Total interest paid; Total payments. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Current card balance: 80000; Annual percentage rate (APR): 36; Fixed monthly payment: 8000. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Why can a card balance take so long to clear?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance. If your payment is only slightly above the interest charged, little money reduces the principal.

Does this include new spending or card fees?

No. Stop adding new purchases for this estimate to be meaningful; fees, rate changes and issuer rules can alter the actual payoff schedule.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

Credit-card payoff: payment must exceed monthly interest

A credit-card payoff estimate applies the assumed APR to the outstanding balance and subtracts a fixed monthly payment. It is especially important to check whether the payment exceeds the interest charged; otherwise the balance may not decline. Real card accounts can include new purchases, fees, changing APRs, minimum-payment rules and payment allocation across different balance types, which this simplified model does not include.

Why minimum payments can prolong debt

When the payment is only a little larger than interest and fees, a small portion reduces principal. The remaining balance then continues to accrue interest. Compare a larger fixed payment and see how much time and interest may be saved. Avoid interpreting the output as an exact payoff date if the issuer changes rates or applies different minimum-payment and allocation rules.

A practical payoff plan

List balances, APRs, fees and due dates for every card. Keep required minimums current to avoid penalties, then direct extra payments according to a clear strategy while preserving essential cash reserves. Check the statement for promotional rates and expiry dates. If payments are unaffordable, contact the issuer or a qualified debt counsellor before missed payments compound the problem.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For an ₹80,000 balance at a 36% APR, the simple monthly-rate approximation is 3%, or about ₹2,400 interest in the first month before any payment. A fixed ₹8,000 payment would initially leave roughly ₹74,400 after that month's interest and payment, assuming no new charges or fees. Later interest falls as the balance falls. Confirm the issuer's calculation method and stop adding purchases for the estimate to be useful.
