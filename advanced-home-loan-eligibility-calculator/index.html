What this calculator does

Model eligibility with primary income, co-applicant income, existing obligations, FOIR, rate and tenure. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Eligible EMI = combined monthly income × FOIR − existing obligations; the supported principal is calculated from that EMI.

Inputs

The calculator uses primary income, co-applicant income, FOIR, existing obligations, interest rate and loan term. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Frequently asked questions

Why add co-applicant income?

Some lenders consider eligible co-applicant income, but policies differ.

Can I include bonuses?

Use only income that is realistically documentable and lender-eligible.

Does higher FOIR always mean more eligibility?

Mathematically yes, but lender risk limits are separate.

Advanced home-loan affordability: test multiple constraints

A more detailed eligibility estimate can help compare the impact of income, existing obligations, interest rate and tenure. It still cannot reproduce every lender's underwriting model. Banks may apply different income multipliers, EMI-to-income limits, credit requirements, property valuation rules and age-at-maturity limits. Treat the output as a scenario for budgeting rather than an approval decision, and do not use a maximum estimate as a target to borrow.

Use conservative household inputs

Include all existing monthly debt payments and use income that is dependable after deductions. If you receive bonuses, commissions or self-employment income, test a lower average rather than relying on the strongest month. Account for dependants, insurance, school fees and likely maintenance expenses even if the formula does not include them. The aim is sustainable affordability, not the largest possible principal.

Compare this estimate with lender criteria

Ask lenders how they calculate eligible income, obligations, co-applicant contributions, credit score, property valuation and loan maturity age. Check processing charges, rate-reset rules, insurance conditions and prepayment terms. If the detailed and basic eligibility tools produce different outputs, compare the assumptions field by field instead of assuming the larger figure is more accurate.

Stress-test the result against household changes

Run a conservative case with a higher rate, lower dependable income and all existing monthly obligations included. Consider future costs such as childcare, education, medical expenses, maintenance and retirement contributions that may not be part of a simple eligibility formula. If a co-applicant contributes income, test whether the payment remains manageable if that income is temporarily unavailable. Check the loan's expected end date against the borrower's age and retirement plans. Ask lenders how they treat bonuses, self-employment income, co-borrowers and property valuation. The most useful outcome is a sustainable payment range, not the maximum number a formula can produce. Final eligibility remains subject to the lender's underwriting and current terms.

Separate lender eligibility from personal comfort

Use the estimated eligibility to understand the possible borrowing range, then choose a lower personal ceiling if the maximum leaves too little monthly flexibility. Include expenses that may rise after moving, such as commuting, maintenance, property tax and insurance. If you have variable income, use a conservative average and keep a separate emergency reserve. Compare a shorter loan term with a longer term to see how much total interest changes. Ask the lender to explain any difference between this estimate and its offer, especially treatment of existing obligations, co-applicant income and property valuation. The tool supports planning; it cannot confirm approval or replace the lender's written terms.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For an illustrative monthly take-home income of ₹1,50,000, existing EMIs of ₹25,000 and a planning ceiling of 40% of income, the total EMI budget is ₹60,000 and the room for a new home loan is ₹35,000. A lender may use a different ratio or exclude some income, so this is not an eligibility guarantee. Run the same inputs at a higher interest rate and shorter remaining working period.
