What this calculator does

Estimate an indicative loan amount from property value and LTV, then calculate EMI and interest. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Indicative loan amount = property value × LTV.

Inputs

The calculator uses Property value, Loan-to-value, Annual interest rate, Loan term. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

Frequently asked questions

What is LTV?

Loan-to-value is the loan amount as a percentage of property value.

Is maximum loan guaranteed?

No. Income, credit profile, valuation and lender policy also matter.

What is missing?

Fees, insurance, legal and valuation charges are excluded.

Loan against property: collateral does not remove repayment risk

A loan against property is secured by an asset, but the approved amount and terms depend on the lender's valuation, loan-to-value policy, income assessment, existing liabilities and property documents. This calculator can estimate repayment from the amount, rate and tenure you enter; it cannot predict sanction, valuation or legal eligibility. Default can put the pledged property at risk, so compare the repayment with household cash flow before borrowing.

What affects the eligible amount?

Lenders may consider property type, marketable value, ownership and title checks, location, existing encumbrances, borrower income and credit history. A percentage of property value is not a guarantee of approval. Co-owners may need to participate in the transaction, and restrictions can apply to certain properties or uses of funds. Confirm all requirements with the lender and legal adviser.

Assess repayment risk separately

The property is collateral, not a substitute for stable repayment capacity. Stress-test the instalment at a higher rate or lower income, and keep emergency funds separate from the loan proceeds. Compare the effective annual cost, prepayment rules, default charges and disbursement conditions. Avoid borrowing the maximum available amount if it leaves too little room for essential spending or unexpected costs.

Questions to ask before pledging property

Ask the lender for the effective annual borrowing cost, processing and legal fees, valuation method, repayment schedule, prepayment terms and consequences of missed payments. Confirm who must sign if the property has co-owners and whether the title documents meet the lender's requirements. Understand whether the funds can be used for your intended purpose and whether disbursement is staged or conditional. Do not assume that the market value you expect is the value the lender will accept. A secured loan can have a lower rate than some unsecured credit, but the collateral increases the consequences of default. Keep the repayment affordable under a higher-rate or lower-income scenario and seek independent legal review where needed.

Review security and default clauses

Read the loan agreement for the definition of default, cure period, late-payment charges, enforcement process and any restrictions on selling or transferring the pledged property. Confirm whether the lender requires insurance or periodic document updates. If the property is jointly owned or already has a mortgage, the lender may require consents or reject the collateral. Ask how the rate can change and whether the loan is callable under any conditions. Do not compare only the rate with an unsecured loan: legal and valuation costs, loan size, flexibility and the risk to the property all matter. Consider independent advice before pledging a home used by your family.

Worked example with Indian rupees

If a lender were to approve ₹20,00,000 at an illustrative 11% annual rate for 10 years, the standard reducing-balance EMI would be about ₹27,550 per month. That estimate excludes processing charges, valuation and legal fees, insurance and other contract terms. The approved amount may be lower than expected if the lender's eligible property value or LTV cap is lower. Obtain a written offer and repayment schedule before relying on the number.
