What this calculator compares

Compare two choices for the same cash available today and the same monthly payment budget. One path invests the available cash upfront and continues the original loan EMI. The other applies the cash to loan principal, keeps the original EMI unchanged, and invests that EMI after the loan is paid off.

How the calculation works

The calculator estimates the original monthly loan payment and simulates the loan balance month by month. In the prepayment path, interest is charged on the reduced balance; once the loan is cleared, the original EMI is invested for the remaining months. In the invest-instead path, the available cash is invested upfront and the original EMI continues to service the loan. Both paths use the same upfront cash, monthly cash budget and remaining-term horizon. Payments and redirected contributions are treated as month-end cash flows.

Inputs

Enter the outstanding loan balance, annual loan rate, remaining term, cash available now and an assumed annual investment return. The cash available now is a one-time amount, not a recurring monthly SIP.

Important assumptions and limitations

The model assumes a fixed nominal loan rate divided by 12, monthly interest accrual, a constant original EMI and a constant monthly investment return. It keeps the EMI unchanged after prepayment so the loan is paid off sooner. It excludes lender-specific daily interest, payment-date rules, rounding, rate resets, prepayment fees, investment taxes, expense ratios, transaction costs and market volatility. Actual investment returns are not guaranteed. Confirm lender terms and test conservative or negative return assumptions; preserve an emergency fund before prepaying.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a recurring SIP projection?

No. The invest-instead path invests the available amount once, upfront. The prepayment path invests the original EMI only after the loan is cleared.

Does the model reduce EMI after prepayment?

No. It keeps the original EMI unchanged so the loan is paid off sooner, then redirects that same monthly cash amount into the investment account.

Are taxes, fees and market volatility included?

No. The return is a constant assumption for comparison. Actual taxes, fees, lender terms and investment outcomes can differ.
