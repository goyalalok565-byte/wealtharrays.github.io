Simple Interest

What this calculator does

The compound interest calculator shows how a starting amount can grow when interest is added to the balance and future interest is then calculated on that larger balance. You can use it to explore the relationship between principal, rate, compounding frequency and time without relying on a spreadsheet.

The result is a mathematical projection, not a promise from a bank, investment or other financial product. Real accounts can use different compounding conventions, rates, fees, taxes and payment schedules.

How compound interest is calculated

The standard model is A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where P is the starting principal, r is the annual rate expressed as a decimal, n is the number of compounding periods per year, and t is the number of years. The interest earned is A − P. More frequent compounding can increase the mathematical result when the nominal rate and other assumptions remain the same.

For recurring contributions, the mathematics is different because money enters the account at different times. Use the SIP or recurring-deposit calculator when regular deposits are the central part of the scenario.

Worked example

Imagine $10,000 is left invested for 10 years at an assumed annual rate of 6% with annual compounding. The calculator applies the formula to show the projected ending balance and separates the original principal from the mathematical growth. Change the time period while keeping the rate fixed to see why compounding becomes more noticeable over longer horizons.

Why the assumptions matter

A calculator can produce an exact answer to an assumed formula while the underlying assumption remains uncertain. A constant 6% rate is a convenient mathematical example; real investment returns may change every year. Likewise, a savings product may quote a rate but apply specific rules for compounding, withdrawals or fees.

For planning, compare multiple rates and periods. If inflation matters to your decision, compare the projected nominal amount with the purchasing power of that amount in the future.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays calculates the selected scenario in your browser. It does not connect to your bank or investment account and does not fetch a live market rate. Taxes, fees, inflation, deposits and withdrawals are not included unless the tool specifically provides those inputs.

Frequently asked questions

How is compound interest different from simple interest?

Simple interest calculates interest from the original principal, while compound interest allows previous interest to become part of the balance on which later interest is calculated.

Does compounding frequency matter?

It can. With the same nominal rate and principal, more frequent compounding can produce a different mathematical result. The actual product terms still control what happens in a real account.

Does this show inflation-adjusted growth?

No. The standard result is nominal. Inflation can reduce purchasing power, so a long-term plan should consider both nominal growth and real purchasing power.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. The result is guaranteed only as a calculation of the assumptions entered. Real rates and account conditions can differ.
