Simple Interest

How it's calculated

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where P is the principal, r is the annual interest rate, n is how many times per year interest compounds, and t is time in years.

Worked example

With initial amount of 5000, annual interest rate of 6%, time period of 10yrs, compounding frequency of monthly, this works out to a principal of $5,000.00, interest earned of $4,096.98, final amount of $9,096.98. Try it yourself in the calculator above with your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from simple interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus any interest already earned, so it grows faster the longer it runs.

Does compounding frequency matter much?

It matters more at higher rates and longer time periods. Moving from annual to monthly compounding usually makes a modest difference; the effect compounds itself over decades.

Does this show real (inflation-adjusted) growth?

No — this is nominal growth. If you want to compare against inflation, subtract your expected inflation rate from the interest rate before entering it.
