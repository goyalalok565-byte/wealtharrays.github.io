What this calculator does

Combine property price, registration, stamp duty, furnishing and other one-time costs to estimate the cash required to buy a home. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

All-in purchase cost = property price + stamp duty + registration + furnishing + other one-time costs.

Inputs

The calculator uses Flat / apartment price, Stamp duty, Registration / transfer, Furnishing and setup, Other one-time costs. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

Frequently asked questions

Are stamp-duty rates universal?

No. Rates vary by jurisdiction, property type and transaction details.

Does this include the home loan?

It estimates purchase costs; financing costs such as loan interest are separate.

Why include furnishing?

A purchase can require substantial cash after handover, so include realistic setup costs.

Estimate the full cost of buying a flat

A flat purchase budget is broader than the advertised base price. Depending on the transaction, the buyer may need to budget for parking, floor-rise or amenity charges, GST where applicable, stamp duty, registration, legal checks, brokerage, interiors and moving costs. Which charges apply depends on location, property status, agreement terms and current law. Use the calculator as a planning checklist, then verify every line item with the builder, seller, registrar and a qualified adviser before signing.

Separate one-time and ongoing costs

One-time acquisition costs can include stamp duty, registration, brokerage, legal review and fit-out. Ongoing costs can include maintenance, property tax, insurance, utilities and loan interest. Keeping these categories separate avoids underestimating the cash required at possession and the monthly cost of ownership. If you are comparing two properties, apply the same cost categories to both so that a lower base price does not hide higher additional charges.

Verify local charges before committing

Stamp duty and registration rates vary by state and can depend on ownership type, transaction value and concessions. GST treatment can differ for under-construction and completed properties under applicable rules. Do not apply a generic percentage as a legal conclusion. Ask for a written cost sheet, confirm whether each charge is included in the agreement value and check official state guidance for duties and registration.

A document checklist for the final cost sheet

Request a written cost sheet that separates agreement value, taxes, stamp duty, registration, parking, maintenance deposits, utility connection charges and any builder or society fees. Ask which amounts are refundable and which are payable before possession. For under-construction property, verify the applicable GST treatment rather than assuming the same rule applies to a completed resale property. Check whether the quoted carpet area, built-up area and agreement value use consistent definitions. If a home loan is involved, add lender processing, valuation and legal charges outside the builder's price. Save invoices and receipts for each payment. This calculator is most reliable when estimates are replaced with documented charges for the exact property and location.

Plan for delays and post-possession spending

A property budget should include the time between booking, loan disbursement, possession and moving in. Delays can create overlapping rent and loan payments or require temporary accommodation. After possession, repairs, appliances, interiors and society deposits can arrive together, so keep a separate contingency rather than allocating the entire savings balance to the down payment. Ask whether the cost sheet includes meter connections, maintenance deposits, parking and any applicable tax. For resale properties, budget for document checks and transfer fees. A conservative all-in budget makes it easier to compare a slightly more expensive property with fewer additional charges against a lower-priced option with uncertain costs.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For an illustrative flat price of ₹75,00,000, suppose additional costs total 8% of the base price. Those additions would be ₹6,00,000, making the estimated acquisition budget ₹81,00,000 before any items not included in that 8% assumption. The percentage is only a scenario, not a standard rate. Enter actual quoted charges individually wherever possible, then add a contingency for repairs, furnishing or delayed possession expenses.
