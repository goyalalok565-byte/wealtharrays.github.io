## Overview

Compound annual growth rate (CAGR) expresses the constant annual rate that links a starting value to an ending value over a stated number of years. It smooths the path into one annualised number; it does not show volatility or the sequence of gains and losses. It is useful for comparing growth periods only when the start, end and time basis are defined consistently.

## How the estimate works

CAGR = [(ending value ÷ starting value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1] × 100. The formula requires a positive starting value, non-negative ending value and positive duration. CAGR is undefined when the starting value is zero.

## Worked examples

- Value rises from ₹100,000 to ₹160,000 over 5 years: CAGR ≈ 9.86% per year.
- Value rises from ₹250,000 to ₹400,000 over 4 years: CAGR ≈ 12.47% per year.

## Frequently asked questions

- **Does CAGR mean the investment earned the same return every year?** No. It is a smoothed equivalent rate, not the actual yearly path.
- **Can CAGR be negative?** Yes. An ending value below the starting value produces negative CAGR.
- **Can I calculate CAGR from zero?** No; the starting-value denominator makes the ratio undefined.
- **Does CAGR include cash flows added during the period?** Not correctly for irregular contributions; use an appropriate money-weighted or time-weighted return method.
- **Does CAGR predict the future?** No. It summarises past start/end values only.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not use CAGR for a series with substantial interim deposits or withdrawals unless the method accounts for cash flows. Do not ignore dates, fees, taxes, dividends or volatility. Avoid comparing periods with different lengths without annualising them consistently.
