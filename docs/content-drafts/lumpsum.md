# Lumpsum Investment Calculator

> Content draft only. Not wired into any calculator page or production route. Last reviewed: 2026-11-21. All examples are illustrative arithmetic, not product quotes or personalized advice.

## Overview

A lump-sum projection shows how one initial contribution could grow under a constant assumed annual return. It is a mathematical scenario, not a forecast or promise. Market-linked investments can fall in value, returns vary, and taxes, fees and timing can change the realised result. Use the assumed rate as a sensitivity input rather than a target you expect an investment to deliver.

## How the estimate works

Future value = initial investment × (1 + annual rate)^years. Estimated gain = future value − initial investment. This annual-compounding model does not represent daily NAV movement or a fund’s actual return path.

## Worked examples

- ₹100,000 at an assumed 10% annual return for 10 years: future value ≈ ₹259,374; gain ≈ ₹159,374.
- ₹500,000 at 6% for 5 years: future value ≈ ₹669,113; gain ≈ ₹169,113.

## Frequently asked questions

- **Does a 10% assumption mean I will earn 10%?** No. It is an input for a hypothetical calculation, not a promised return.
- **Is lumpsum riskier than SIP?** A one-time investment exposes the full amount to the market from the start; outcomes depend on timing and asset risk.
- **Are fees included?** Only if the calculator explicitly asks for them; otherwise subtract fees separately.
- **Does this include tax?** No. Tax depends on product, holding period and jurisdiction.
- **Can I compare different time horizons?** Yes, but keep assumptions consistent and remember longer projections compound uncertainty.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Do not treat historical returns as guaranteed future returns. Do not compare a pre-fee projection with an after-fee product quote. Check liquidity, risk level, diversification and whether the investment horizon matches the goal.
