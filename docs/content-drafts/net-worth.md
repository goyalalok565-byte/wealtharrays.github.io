# Net Worth Calculator

> Content draft only. Not wired into any calculator page or production route. Last reviewed: 2026-11-21. All examples are illustrative arithmetic, not product quotes or personalized advice.

## Overview

Net worth is a snapshot of assets minus liabilities at a chosen date. It can help track balance-sheet progress, but it is not the same as cash available to spend or a measure of financial well-being by itself. Use realistic current values, include debts consistently, and avoid counting the same asset or liability twice. For property and private assets, valuation is an estimate rather than a guaranteed sale price.

## How the estimate works

Net worth = total assets − total liabilities. Assets may include cash, investments, property and other owned items; liabilities may include mortgages, personal loans, card balances and other amounts owed. Use one valuation date and one currency basis.

## Worked examples

- Assets: cash ₹250,000, investments ₹400,000, property ₹1,200,000; liabilities ₹600,000 → net worth ₹1,250,000.
- Assets ₹900,000 and liabilities ₹1,050,000 → net worth −₹150,000.

## Frequently asked questions

- **Should I include my home?** Yes, if you include its estimated current value and the outstanding mortgage separately.
- **Is net worth the same as savings?** No. It includes assets that may be illiquid and subtracts liabilities.
- **How often should I update it?** Use a consistent interval, such as quarterly or annually, and the same valuation rules.
- **Should I include credit-card balances?** Yes, include amounts owed as liabilities to avoid overstating net worth.
- **Can two people with the same net worth have different financial security?** Yes. Liquidity, income stability, obligations and goals also matter.

## Common mistakes to avoid

Avoid double-counting joint assets or retirement balances. Do not list a home’s full value and forget the mortgage. Use conservative valuations for illiquid assets and keep the valuation date visible.
