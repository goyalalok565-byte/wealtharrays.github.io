What this calculator does

Estimate fixed monthly payments and total borrowing cost for a personal loan. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

For a fixed-rate loan, EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of payments.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

Personal-loan affordability should be checked against both EMI and total repayment. Keep the principal fixed while testing rates, then keep the rate fixed while testing terms so you can identify what is actually changing the borrowing cost.

Check the lender's disclosed annual rate, processing fee, insurance or other mandatory charges and prepayment conditions. A calculator can model the amortization formula, but only the actual loan agreement determines the transaction cost.

Worked scenario

Enter ₹5,00,000, 12% and 5 years as the baseline. Then keep the loan amount fixed and compare 10%, 12% and 14%. This isolates the effect of the interest rate. Next compare a 3-year term: the payment is higher, but the interest burden is lower. Use the lender's sanctioned schedule to verify the actual figures before accepting an offer.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between interest rate and APR?

APR aims to include the annual cost of credit and relevant charges; use the applicable lender disclosure.

Does this include a processing fee?

Not unless separately entered; a fee can increase the effective cost.

Why does a longer term reduce EMI?

More instalments spread principal repayment over a longer period, often increasing total interest.

Can I repay early?

Check the contract for prepayment terms and charges.

Is this an approval estimate?

No. Lender underwriting and eligibility rules are separate.

Formula and assumptions for this calculator

For a fixed-rate amortizing personal loan, EMI is calculated from the principal, monthly interest rate and number of monthly payments. The calculator separates total repayment from the original principal to show estimated interest.

Overview and scope

A personal-loan estimate models a fixed principal repaid in equal instalments at a constant rate. It is a planning calculation, not an offer or eligibility decision. Processing fees, insurance, late charges, rate resets and disbursement deductions can change the effective cost. Compare the lender’s disclosed APR and repayment schedule before accepting credit.

Calculation method and assumptions

For a standard amortising loan, EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ [(1+r)^n − 1], where r is the periodic interest rate and n is the number of payments. At zero interest, EMI = P ÷ n. Total interest = n × EMI − P.

Additional worked examples

  • Borrow ₹500,000 at 12% nominal annual interest for 60 months: EMI ≈ ₹11,122.22; total repayment ≈ ₹667,333.43; interest ≈ ₹167,333.43.
  • Borrow ₹120,000 at 0% for 24 months: EMI = ₹5,000; total interest = ₹0.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not compare loans by EMI alone. Check net disbursed amount, all fees, APR, payment dates and prepayment conditions. Avoid entering a promotional rate as though it were a guaranteed personal offer.
