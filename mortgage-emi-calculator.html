Mortgage / Loan EMI

What this calculator does

The EMI calculator estimates a regular loan payment from the principal, annual interest rate and repayment period. It is useful for understanding how a change in borrowing amount, rate or tenure affects the payment and the total amount repaid.

The result is a generic amortization estimate. Actual lenders can apply different fees, payment dates, rate structures, insurance requirements, rounding rules and eligibility conditions.

How the EMI formula works

For a standard fixed-rate amortizing loan, the payment is calculated as EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where P is principal, r is the periodic interest rate and n is the number of payments. The annual rate must be converted to the payment-period rate before applying the formula.

Each payment contains both interest and principal. Early payments can contain a larger interest component because interest is calculated on a larger outstanding balance. As principal is repaid, the interest component normally declines.

Worked example

Suppose you borrow $20,000 for five years and enter an assumed annual rate of 8%. The calculator estimates the regular payment and total repayment under the standard amortization model. Change the term from five years to three or seven years to see the trade-off between monthly cash flow and total interest.

How to compare loan scenarios

A lower monthly payment is not automatically a lower-cost loan. Extending the repayment period can reduce each payment while increasing the total interest paid. Compare both the monthly payment and the total repayment before judging whether a loan structure fits your budget.

Also check whether the quoted rate is fixed, variable or promotional. A generic calculator cannot reproduce every lender-specific condition.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses the standard amortization formula in your browser. It does not access lender systems, approve loans or include product-specific charges unless they are explicitly represented by inputs. Actual repayment schedules can differ because of fees, taxes, payment timing and contract terms.

Frequently asked questions

Why is early loan interest higher?

Interest is calculated from the outstanding principal. At the beginning of an amortizing loan, the outstanding balance is largest, so the interest component of the payment can also be larger.

Does paying extra principal help?

Extra principal can reduce the balance on which future interest is calculated, but whether it is permitted and how much it saves depends on the lender and contract.

Does this handle every type of mortgage?

No. The standard calculator models a regular amortizing payment. Adjustable rates, interest-only periods, balloon payments and special lender rules may require a different model.

Does the calculator include fees?

Not automatically. Check processing fees, insurance, taxes, prepayment charges and other costs separately when evaluating a real loan.

Before using a lender's quote

Compare the calculator's principal, rate and tenure with the lender's actual offer. Check whether the quoted rate is annual or periodic, whether it can change, and which fees are charged separately. Two loans with the same EMI can have different total costs when their terms or fees differ.
