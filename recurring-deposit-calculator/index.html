What this calculator does

Estimate the maturity value of regular monthly deposits using an assumed annual rate. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

A recurring-deposit projection is the future value of a stream of equal deposits. Each deposit has a different amount of time to earn interest, so the result is the sum of the compounded deposits.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

The key RD trade-off is contribution size versus time. Because deposits enter at different dates, do not compare an RD maturity value with a lump-sum deposit without accounting for how much capital was actually invested and for how long.

For an Indian RD, verify installment timing, missed-payment rules, compounding convention, tax treatment and premature closure with the bank. Use the calculator to compare scenarios, not to reproduce a bank's contractual maturity statement.

Worked scenario

At ₹5,000 per month, 7% and 5 years, the calculator makes 60 monthly deposits totaling ₹3,00,000. Run a second scenario at 5 years but with ₹7,500 per month: the difference comes mainly from your contribution rate. When comparing an actual RD, confirm deposit timing, compounding method, tax treatment and premature-closure rules with the bank.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

What does the inflation result mean?

It estimates how much money may be needed in the future to have the same purchasing power as today's amount under the constant inflation rate entered.

Is a constant inflation rate realistic?

It is a simplifying assumption. Actual inflation changes, so use several rates to understand a range rather than treating one projection as a forecast.

How does inflation affect retirement planning?

Higher inflation increases the future cost of today's lifestyle and can raise the savings target. Pair the result with spending and investment-return scenarios.

Does this predict a specific item's price?

No. It is a broad mathematical model. Individual goods and services can rise faster or slower than the general inflation assumption.

Recurring-deposit example: ₹5,000 a month for 5 years

Imagine depositing ₹5,000 at the end of every month for 5 years at an assumed 7% annual rate. You contribute ₹3,00,000 in total, and the model estimates roughly ₹3,60,000 before tax, depending on the exact compounding convention. The important comparison is contribution total versus modeled interest, not just the final maturity number.

Formula and assumptions for this calculator

Future value = P × [((1 + i)^n − 1) ÷ i], where P is the monthly deposit, i is the monthly rate and n is the whole number of monthly deposits. This model assumes each deposit is made at the end of the month and compounds monthly.

How to validate your inputs

Use the published example inputs as a baseline: Monthly deposit: 1000; Annual interest rate: 7; Deposit term: 5. Confirm the unit, period and eligibility conditions for each input against your own documents or the relevant provider. Change one assumption at a time and compare the output with an independent statement, invoice or schedule before making a material decision.
