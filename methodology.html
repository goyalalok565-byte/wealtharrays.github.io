METHODOLOGY

How Wealth Arrays calculators work

Wealth Arrays is built around a simple principle: a calculator should show the reasoning behind a number, not only display the number. Our tools are educational planning utilities. They perform defined mathematical calculations in the browser from the inputs you provide and explain the main assumptions that affect the result.

1. Formula first, result second

Each calculator is based on a documented mathematical model. Investment tools use formulas for compounding, future value, recurring contributions or annualized growth. Loan tools use amortization mathematics where appropriate. Business, income and percentage tools use the definitions described on their individual pages.

We aim to keep formulas understandable enough that a user can reproduce the logic independently. Where a formula has a special timing convention, such as a contribution occurring at the beginning or end of a period, the calculator page should state that convention. A mathematically correct formula can still produce an unsuitable planning result when the assumptions do not match the real situation.

2. Assumptions are not predictions

Inputs such as annual return, inflation, interest rate, working hours, future spending and time horizon are assumptions unless a tool explicitly says otherwise. Changing an assumption can materially change the output. For long-term planning, we recommend comparing several scenarios rather than treating one precise-looking result as a forecast.

Real financial products can also have fees, taxes, minimums, eligibility rules, payment timing, rounding conventions and other conditions that a generic calculator cannot know. When a product-specific rule matters, the provider's current documentation or the applicable authority should take precedence over a generic estimate.

3. Browser-first calculations

The calculator arithmetic is performed in your browser from the values entered into the tool. Wealth Arrays does not need access to your bank or investment account to calculate a scenario. This design also means that identical inputs should produce the same deterministic mathematical output when the same model and rounding rules are used.

Currency selection generally changes the displayed currency symbol and formatting. It does not mean that the calculator has performed a live foreign-exchange conversion unless the specific tool explicitly provides such a conversion.

4. Worked examples and interpretation

Calculator pages include worked examples or scenario guidance so that the result has context. A good example should show what the inputs mean, what the formula is doing and why the result should not be interpreted as a guarantee. Users should be able to change an assumption and understand why the output changed.

5. Accuracy checks

We test formulas against known mathematical scenarios and review reported problems. A useful bug report includes the calculator name, the exact inputs entered, the displayed result and what you expected instead. This makes it possible to reproduce the issue rather than relying on a screenshot or an approximate description.

Software correctness and financial correctness are different questions. A calculator can implement its formula correctly while the formula itself is not appropriate for a particular product or jurisdiction. Our methodology therefore separates arithmetic accuracy from product-specific advice.

6. Tax, loan and investment limitations

Tax rules can change by country, state, filing status and tax year. Loan contracts can include fees, variable rates and special repayment rules. Investments can have volatile returns, taxes and costs. Wealth Arrays calculators are not official government calculators, lender underwriting systems or personalised investment advice.

For decisions that depend on current regulations, contract terms or personal circumstances, verify the relevant details with the official source or an appropriately qualified professional.

7. Corrections and transparency

We prefer correcting a reproducible error at its source rather than masking a symptom on a single page. When a formula, explanation or technical implementation is found to be wrong, the relevant source is updated and the production build is regenerated so the correction is not lost on a later deployment.

If you find an issue, send the calculator name, inputs and expected result to goyalalok565@gmail.com. Suggestions about unclear explanations are also welcome because usability is part of calculator quality.

Not financial, tax or legal advice

Wealth Arrays provides educational estimates and explanations. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, inflation, timing, market movements, regulations, eligibility rules and other factors. Use the tools to understand a calculation, then verify important decisions using current authoritative information.
