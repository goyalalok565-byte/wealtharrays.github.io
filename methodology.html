METHODOLOGY

How Wealth Arrays calculators work

Wealth Arrays treats a calculator as a transparent mathematical model, not a prediction engine. This page documents the formulas, timing conventions, assumptions, limitations and correction process so users can understand where a result comes from.

What “accurate” means

There are two separate questions: arithmetic accuracy and model suitability. Arithmetic accuracy means the implementation produces the expected mathematical result for stated inputs. Model suitability asks whether those assumptions match a particular bank product, loan contract, tax regime or investment situation. Wealth Arrays can test the first; it cannot guarantee the second.

Timing, compounding and rounding

Timing can materially change a result. A recurring contribution at the beginning of a period earns one additional period of growth compared with a period-end contribution. Loan products can also use day-count, payment-date and lender-specific rounding rules. Where Wealth Arrays uses a simplified convention, the calculator documentation should state it rather than implying an exact product quote.

Calculations use numeric inputs before display formatting. Currency values are rounded for readability, so reverse-engineering a formula from a rounded headline can produce a small difference. For contractual payments, deposits or tax filings, use the provider's or authority's official figure.

Inflation-adjusted results

Where a calculator shows today's purchasing power, the nominal result is divided by (1 + inflation)^years. This is a planning transformation, not a forecast and not a currency conversion.

Testing and reproducibility

Calculator definitions, page content and runtime code are maintained in the source repository and deployed through a repeatable build pipeline. Testing covers ordinary cases, zero-rate cases, boundary inputs and known mathematical relationships where applicable. Reported bugs are investigated from exact inputs rather than patched only in rendered HTML, so corrections survive later deployments.

What these models leave out

Generic calculators cannot automatically know every fee, tax, eligibility rule, market movement, payment holiday, prepayment penalty, product-specific compounding rule or contract condition. A result can therefore be mathematically correct while unsuitable as a real-world quote.

Retirement and withdrawal-rate assumptions

The retirement calculator is a scenario tool. Historical research has examined sustainable withdrawal rates, but no single percentage guarantees that a portfolio will last. Test different spending, withdrawal-rate and return assumptions rather than treating one target as certain.

Corrections and change control

When a formula, explanation or implementation is found to be wrong, the underlying source is corrected and the production bundle is regenerated. We do not alter formulas to produce a preferred-looking result. Send the calculator, exact inputs, displayed result and expected result or source of the discrepancy to goyalalok565@gmail.com.

Use authoritative information for decisions

For current tax rules, loan contracts, deposit terms, investment charges or regulatory requirements, the relevant government, regulator, lender, bank, fund provider or other authoritative document takes precedence over a generic educational calculator.
