Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

General loan EMI: compare instalment and total borrowing cost

A standard EMI estimate assumes a fixed annual rate, regular monthly payments and a reducing principal balance. It can be used for many amortising loans, but it is not a substitute for a lender's repayment schedule. Flat-rate loans, balloon payments, introductory rates, fees financed into the principal and changing interest rates can produce different cash flows. Enter the amount actually borrowed, not automatically the sticker price of the purchase.

EMI formula and assumptions

For principal P, monthly rate r and n monthly instalments, EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1). The monthly rate is commonly the annual nominal rate divided by 12; the exact convention should match the lender's disclosure. At a zero rate, the payment reduces to principal divided by the number of instalments. The formula assumes regular payments and does not automatically include fees, insurance, late charges or rate changes.

Compare offers on a like-for-like basis

Use the same loan amount, term and repayment frequency for each offer. Compare total repayment, fees, prepayment conditions and whether the quoted rate is fixed or floating. A lower EMI can simply reflect a longer term and may cost more in total interest. If the payment is close to your monthly budget limit, test a higher rate and a temporary income reduction before taking the loan.

How to read the amortisation trade-off

At the start of a reducing-balance loan, interest is calculated on a relatively large outstanding principal. As scheduled payments reduce that balance, the interest component generally falls and more of each instalment goes toward principal. Extending the term can lower the monthly instalment while increasing the number of months on which interest is charged. Extra payments may shorten the term or reduce future instalments depending on lender policy. Ask for a repayment schedule and compare the total amount payable, not just the first EMI. If the rate can reset, run a higher-rate scenario. The estimate excludes contract-specific charges unless you include them in the amount borrowed or model them separately.

Understand the schedule before signing

Ask the lender to show the opening balance, interest charged, principal repaid and closing balance for several early instalments. This makes it easier to confirm that the calculator's reducing-balance assumptions match the actual contract. Check whether interest starts on disbursement or another date and whether partial disbursement creates pre-EMI interest. If a loan is secured against a purchase, make sure financed fees are reflected in the principal used for the estimate. When comparing offers, use the same principal and tenure and read the Key Facts Statement or equivalent disclosure to understand the total amount payable and charges.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For a ₹5,00,000 loan at an illustrative 12% annual rate for 5 years, the reducing-balance EMI is approximately ₹11,122 per month. Across 60 payments, total repayment is about ₹6.67 lakh and modeled interest is around ₹1.67 lakh before fees. Compare the lender's APR and amortisation schedule against this estimate. A processing fee or compulsory add-on may increase the effective borrowing cost even if the stated interest rate matches.
