What this calculator does

Estimate how inflation could change future prices and purchasing power. The result is generated locally from the values you choose, so the most useful way to use the tool is to test realistic assumptions instead of looking for a single “correct” future number.

Good financial planning is usually a range exercise. Try a conservative scenario, a middle scenario and a stressful scenario. The comparison feature is intended to make those trade-offs visible without opening separate calculators.

How the calculation works

Future cost = today's amount × (1 + inflation rate)^years. This is a scenario model: real inflation varies over time and can differ by spending category.

The formula is deliberately transparent because a calculator should explain the logic behind the answer. A precise-looking number is not automatically a precise prediction: the mathematics can be correct while the assumptions are unrealistic. Keep those two ideas separate.

How to use the result responsibly

Inflation is most useful as a range, not one exact forecast. Run 4%, 6% and 8% scenarios to see how a long horizon changes the amount needed for the same purchasing power.

For a retirement or education goal, connect the future-cost result to an income or investment plan. A future price estimate by itself does not tell you whether your savings will keep pace with inflation.

Worked scenario

Enter ₹1,00,000, 6% inflation and 20 years. The calculator estimates that you may need about ₹3.21 lakh in 2046 to buy what ₹1 lakh buys today under that constant-rate assumption. Now test 4% and 8% to see the range. For retirement planning, compare this future spending requirement with expected income and investment growth rather than using inflation in isolation.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays uses deterministic formulas: identical inputs produce identical outputs. Real-world outcomes may differ because financial products can use different day-count conventions, compounding schedules, payment timing, fees, taxes, rounding rules and eligibility conditions. Where a country-specific rule matters, a generic calculator should not pretend to know it.

This page therefore presents the result as an estimate for education and planning. Check official documents and current rules before taking action based on a calculation.

Frequently asked questions

Is the inflation rate the same everywhere?

No. It varies by country, region, basket and time period.

Does CPI measure every household’s costs?

No. It measures a defined index population and basket.

Is the rate guaranteed for future years?

No. A constant rate is a scenario assumption.

Why can my personal inflation feel higher?

Your spending mix may differ from the official index basket.

Does this calculate investment returns?

No. It only adjusts amounts for an assumed price-level change.

Overview and scope

Inflation changes what a given amount can buy over time. This calculator applies a user-entered average rate to show a hypothetical future price level or the present purchasing power of a future amount. Actual inflation varies by country, household basket and period. Official price indexes such as CPI and PCE measure defined populations and baskets; they are not identical to an individual’s personal inflation rate.

Calculation method and assumptions

Future equivalent = current amount × (1 + annual inflation rate)^years. Present purchasing power = future amount ÷ (1 + annual inflation rate)^years. This constant-rate model assumes the same rate every year.

Additional worked examples

  • ₹100,000 growing at an assumed 6% inflation for 10 years has a future equivalent of about ₹179,085.
  • ₹250,000 received in 5 years at an assumed 4% inflation has present purchasing power of about ₹205,480.

Common mistakes to avoid

Do not confuse nominal growth with real purchasing-power growth. Do not use a US CPI series as an India or global inflation measure. State the index, geography, reference dates and whether the rate is an assumption or official observation.
