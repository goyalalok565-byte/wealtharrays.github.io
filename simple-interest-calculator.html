What this calculator does

The simple interest calculator estimates interest from an original principal, an annual rate and a time period. Unlike compound interest, the standard simple-interest model calculates interest from the original principal rather than repeatedly adding interest to the balance.

This makes the model easy to understand and useful for teaching, quick estimates and situations where simple interest is explicitly part of the product or agreement. It should not be used automatically for every savings or borrowing product.

How simple interest is calculated

The core formula is I = P × r × t, where P is principal, r is the annual rate as a decimal and t is time in years. The ending amount is A = P + I. For example, $5,000 at 6% simple interest for two years produces $600 of interest and a mathematical ending amount of $5,600 before any other charges or taxes.

The formula assumes a constant rate and a straightforward time period. Actual contracts can use different day-count conventions, payment schedules or fees.

Worked example

Enter $5,000 as the principal, 6% as the annual rate and two years as the term. The calculator shows the interest separately from the principal so the result is easy to audit. Change the rate or time while keeping the other inputs fixed to see how each assumption affects the result.

Simple interest versus compound interest

Under simple interest, interest does not itself become part of the principal used to calculate later interest. Under compound interest, previous interest can increase the balance and therefore the next interest calculation. Over longer periods, the difference can become significant.

For a real product, use the method stated in the agreement rather than choosing a formula because it produces a preferred result.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays runs this calculation locally in the browser. It does not retrieve rates from banks or lenders and does not decide which interest convention applies to a contract. Taxes, fees and special payment rules are outside the basic formula.

Frequently asked questions

Where is simple interest used?

It can appear in certain loans, short-term agreements, educational examples and other arrangements. The actual contract should determine which calculation method applies.

Do credit cards normally use simple interest?

Credit-card interest can involve daily balances, periodic rates, grace periods and other rules. Do not assume the simple-interest formula matches a card statement.

Which is better, simple or compound interest?

Neither formula is inherently better. They describe different calculation methods. What matters is which method and terms apply to the actual financial product.

Are taxes and fees included?

No, unless explicitly represented by an input. Check the terms of the relevant product or agreement for additional costs.

Check the agreement before choosing the formula

The word “interest” does not by itself tell you whether a contract uses simple or compound mathematics. Read the product terms for the stated rate, calculation frequency, payment timing and additional charges. The calculator is most reliable when its assumptions match those documented terms.
