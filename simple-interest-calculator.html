Compound Interest

How it's calculated

SI = (P × R × T) / 100, where P is the principal, R is the annual interest rate as a percentage, and T is the time in years.

Worked example

With principal amount of 5000, annual interest rate of 8%, time period of 2yrs, this works out to a interest of $800.00, total payable of $5,800.00. Try it yourself in the calculator above with your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Where is simple interest actually used?

It's common for short-term loans, some certificates of deposit, and basic consumer credit agreements — anywhere interest is calculated once on the original amount rather than recalculated on a growing balance.

Do credit cards use simple interest?

Most credit cards actually compound daily, which behaves more like the compound interest calculator on this site — check your card's terms rather than assuming.

Which is better for a saver — simple or compound?

Compound interest earns more over time for a saver, since interest is added to the balance and itself earns interest. Simple interest earns more for a borrower, since the interest owed doesn't grow on itself.
