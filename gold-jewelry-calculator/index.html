What this calculator does

Estimate gold jewellery price from rate, weight, purity, wastage, making charges and GST. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Fine-gold value = rate per gram × weight × purity; wastage, making charges and tax are then added.

Inputs

The calculator uses gold rate, weight, purity, wastage, making charge and tax. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Gold jewellery price: purity and making charges matter

A gold-jewellery estimate combines the gold rate, net metal weight, purity and retail charges. The quoted rate may refer to 24-karat gold while jewellery is commonly sold at a lower purity such as 22K or 18K. Making charges, wastage policies, stones and applicable GST can materially affect the bill. Confirm the rate basis and the exact net gold weight before comparing a displayed estimate with a jeweller's invoice.

Avoid double-counting purity

First identify whether the entered price is for 24K, 22K or another purity. If the quote already reflects the jewellery purity, multiplying by the purity fraction again will understate the metal value. Use net gold weight rather than gross weight when stones or other materials are present. Ask for hallmark details and an itemised invoice.

Making charges and resale deductions

Making charges may be calculated per gram or as a percentage, and policies vary by retailer. Stones and design charges may not be recovered at resale, and buy-back deductions can differ from the purchase invoice. The calculator does not verify live rates, purity or authenticity. Check the current rate and jeweller's written terms on the day of purchase.

Validate the rate, purity and making-charge basis

Ask whether the displayed gold price is a 24K reference rate or a rate already adjusted for 22K, 18K or another purity. Confirm the net gold weight separately from stones, beads or other materials. Making charges may be calculated per gram or as a percentage, and a quoted wastage amount should be itemised rather than assumed. Check hallmark and purity information and request a detailed invoice showing metal value, labour, tax and other fees. A retail purchase price is not the same as a buy-back value: design charges and taxes may not be recovered on resale. The calculator does not verify authenticity or live rates, so use the jeweller's current written quote for the final comparison.

Compare the final invoice rather than the display rate

Two jewellers may quote similar gold rates but calculate making charges, wastage and discounts differently. Request an itemised invoice showing purity, net gold weight, metal rate, labour, tax and any stone value. Confirm whether the per-gram rate is already purity-adjusted. A discount on making charges is not the same as a discount on gold value, and neither necessarily improves resale proceeds. Keep the bill and hallmark details for future exchange or resale. This calculator helps reconcile the arithmetic of a quote but cannot authenticate the item, verify a live rate or predict the buy-back amount that a shop will offer later.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For illustration, 10 grams of 22K gold valued from a 24K reference price of ₹7,000 per gram has a simple purity-adjusted metal value of ₹7,000 × 10 × 22/24 = about ₹64,167 before making charges and taxes. The reference price is illustrative, not live. If the seller already quotes a 22K rate, do not apply the purity adjustment a second time.
