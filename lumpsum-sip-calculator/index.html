What this calculator does

Project wealth when you invest a starting lump sum and continue with monthly contributions under the same return assumption. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Combined future value = lump-sum future value + SIP future value.

Inputs

The calculator uses Initial lump sum, Monthly SIP, Expected annual return, Investment period. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material financial decision.

Frequently asked questions

Why combine lumpsum and SIP?

Many investors make an initial investment and then add money monthly.

Does this guarantee the return?

No. The return is an assumption and real returns vary.

Does it include taxes and fees?

No. Product-specific costs should be considered separately.

Compare a one-time investment with monthly contributions

A lump-sum-versus-SIP comparison shows how contribution timing changes the projected value under a chosen return assumption. A lump sum is invested at the start, while an SIP spreads contributions across months; money invested earlier has more time to compound, but the result depends on the market path and cash-flow assumptions. A constant assumed return is a mathematical scenario, not a forecast of market performance or a recommendation about which method is right for you.

Make the comparison fair

Keep total planned contributions, end date and return convention consistent. Check whether the SIP is treated as beginning-of-month or end-of-month and whether the lump sum is invested immediately. Fees, taxes, missed contributions and the actual sequence of returns can change the result. If you already have the cash available, liquidity needs and risk tolerance matter alongside the modelled value.

Use scenarios rather than predictions

Run low, base and high return assumptions, and include a flat or negative period if the investment is market-linked. The model cannot tell you what the market will do next month. For an actual investment decision, consider diversification, emergency savings, time horizon and product costs, and avoid treating a higher assumed return as evidence that one strategy is inherently superior.

Timing, risk and liquidity are part of the comparison

The arithmetic comparison cannot determine the best entry strategy without knowing the investor's circumstances. A lump sum puts the full amount at market risk immediately, while periodic contributions spread the entry dates. That can change both risk exposure and the realised return, but it does not guarantee that an SIP will outperform or reduce losses in every market. Compare the same total amount and end date, and ensure the assumed return convention is applied consistently. Include product costs, tax, emergency savings and the possibility of needing the money before the target date. For a market-linked investment, run weak, flat and strong return scenarios and treat each as a hypothetical path rather than a prediction.

Match contribution dates and total budget

A useful comparison uses the same total planned contribution and the same final valuation date. If the lump sum is available today but the SIP is spread across future months, those are different cash-flow patterns and should be shown explicitly. If the money is earned monthly rather than already available, an immediate lump-sum comparison may not be realistic. Also consider whether keeping cash uninvested while waiting for SIP dates earns interest and whether fees differ by transaction frequency. A calculator can compare chosen assumptions, but the actual return sequence is unknowable in advance. Keep the decision aligned with your liquidity needs and ability to tolerate losses.

Worked example with Indian rupees

With a ₹1,20,000 budget and a hypothetical 10% annual return over one year, a lump sum grows to about ₹1,32,000 before fees and taxes. Investing the same total as ₹10,000 at the end of each month produces about ₹1,25,656 under a 10% nominal annual rate compounded monthly. The comparison assumes smooth returns and end-of-month SIP payments; actual market returns vary, and different contribution timing changes the result.
