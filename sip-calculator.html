What this calculator does

The SIP calculator estimates how a recurring monthly investment could grow when you choose an assumed annual return and investment period. It separates the amount you contribute from the growth produced by the mathematical assumption, so you can see how much of the final value comes from your own contributions and how much comes from compounding.

A SIP is a contribution pattern, not a guaranteed investment product. The calculator does not know which fund, security or account you will use, and it does not predict market returns. Its purpose is to make the arithmetic of regular investing easier to understand and compare.

How the SIP calculation works

For a regular monthly contribution made at the beginning of each period, the future-value model is FV = P × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i), where P is the monthly contribution, i is the monthly rate, and n is the number of monthly contributions. The monthly rate is derived from the annual rate entered into the calculator. When the rate is zero, the result simply becomes the contribution multiplied by the number of months.

This formula describes a mathematical scenario. It does not mean an investment will actually earn the same return every month or every year. Real investments can have uneven returns, fees, taxes, delays and other costs.

Worked example

Suppose you invest $200 each month for 15 years and enter an assumed annual return of 10%. Your total contributions are $36,000. The calculator then estimates the additional growth produced by the assumed compounding rate. Try changing the monthly amount, period and return separately to see which assumption has the largest effect on the projected value.

How to use the result responsibly

Use at least three scenarios rather than treating one return assumption as a promise. A conservative case, a middle case and a more optimistic case can show how sensitive a long-term target is to the return assumption. Also consider inflation: a future balance may be larger in nominal currency while buying less than the same number represents today.

If you are comparing actual investment products, check their fees, taxes, exit rules and historical disclosures separately. Those details are outside this calculator's generic model.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs the calculation in your browser using deterministic arithmetic. Identical inputs produce the same mathematical result. The model does not forecast market movements, select investments, include product-specific taxes or fees unless explicitly represented by an input, or guarantee an outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Is the SIP return guaranteed?

No. The annual return is an assumption entered for planning. Actual investment returns can be higher, lower or negative and can vary substantially over time.

Does a SIP calculator include inflation?

Not automatically. The displayed future amount is normally a nominal projection. For a realistic long-term plan, compare the result with an inflation-adjusted target as well.

Does it include taxes and fund fees?

No, unless a particular calculator explicitly includes those inputs. Product-specific charges and taxes should be checked against the applicable provider and current rules.

Why should I compare different return assumptions?

Because the return assumption can have a large effect over long periods. Comparing scenarios helps show the range implied by the model instead of presenting one number as a forecast.
