How it's calculated

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) math is the future value of a series of equal monthly payments, compounded monthly: FV = P × [((1+i)^n − 1) / i] × (1+i), where P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of months.

Worked example

With monthly investment of 200, expected annual return of 10%, investment period of 15yrs, this works out to a total invested of $36,000.00, wealth gained of $47,584.85, projected value of $83,584.85. Try it yourself in the calculator above with your own numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A SIP is simply investing a fixed amount at regular intervals — usually monthly — rather than a single lump sum. It's commonly used with mutual funds, but the math applies to any recurring investment.

Is a 10% annual return realistic?

It depends entirely on what you invest in. Equity markets have historically returned around that range over long periods in some markets, but returns are never guaranteed and vary by year — treat any rate you enter as an assumption, not a promise.

Does this account for fees or taxes?

No — this is a gross growth projection. Fund fees, taxes on gains, and inflation will all reduce the real amount you end up with.
