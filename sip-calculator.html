How it's calculated

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) math is the future value of a series of equal monthly payments, compounded monthly: FV = P × [((1+i)^n − 1) / i] × (1+i), where P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the number of months. This model treats each contribution as occurring at the beginning of its monthly period. If the rate is zero, the calculation simplifies to P × n.

Worked example

With a monthly investment of 200, an expected annual return of 10%, and an investment period of 15 years, the model produces total contributions of $36,000.00, projected growth of $47,584.85, and a projected value of $83,584.85. These figures are mathematical projections based on the entered assumption, not a guaranteed investment outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What is a SIP?

A SIP is simply investing a fixed amount at regular intervals — usually monthly — rather than a single lump sum. It's commonly used with mutual funds, but the math applies to any recurring investment.

Is a 10% annual return guaranteed?

No. The annual return entered into this calculator is a planning assumption, not a promise. Test multiple return assumptions rather than relying on a single projected rate.

Does this account for fees or taxes?

No — this is a gross growth projection. Fund fees, taxes on gains, and inflation will all reduce the real amount you end up with.
