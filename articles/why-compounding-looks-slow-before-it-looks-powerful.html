FINANCIAL PLANNING GUIDE

Why Compounding Looks Slow Before It Looks Powerful

Understand why investment growth often feels disappointing early and why time matters more than dramatic assumptions.

The first years can look underwhelming

Compounding works on a growing base. Early on, the base is small, so even a reasonable return may not look dramatic.

Time changes the shape of growth

As the accumulated amount grows, gains can themselves begin contributing to later gains. That does not guarantee a smooth investment return, but it explains why long periods matter in compound-growth scenarios.

Do not solve impatience with unrealistic returns

If a goal looks too far away, increasing the assumed return is usually the easiest way to make the projection attractive—and one of the weakest planning habits. Test a higher contribution or longer time horizon first.

Bottom line

Compounding rewards consistency and time in a mathematical model. The model is useful for planning, not a promise of market performance.
