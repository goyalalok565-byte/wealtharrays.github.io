Step-Up SIP Calculator Guide: How Annual Increases Change the Outcome

Written and maintained by Wealth Arrays. Last reviewed: September 25, 2026. This guide explains a calculation method and is not personalised financial advice.

A Step-Up SIP increases the monthly contribution over time. That simple change can materially alter how much you invest and how much your portfolio may compound.

What is a Step-Up SIP?

Instead of keeping the monthly investment fixed, you increase it at a chosen interval, commonly once a year. For example, a ₹5,000 monthly contribution with a 10% annual step-up becomes ₹5,500 in the second year, then ₹6,050 in the third.

Why the contribution increase matters

There are two separate forces in the projection: the amount you contribute and the return earned on those contributions. A step-up increases the first force while giving later contributions less time to compound than the earliest ones. That is why the result should not be described as “just adding 10% every year.”

Common mistake: choosing an aggressive step-up

A high step-up rate can make a projection look impressive while quietly assuming that your income and savings capacity rise just as quickly. A smaller increase that you can sustain may be a more useful planning scenario.

Compare it with a standard SIP

Use the SIP Calculator with the same starting contribution, return and period. The difference isolates the effect of increasing contributions when you model the higher contributions separately.

Limitations

This is a deterministic projection. Real investments have changing returns, taxes, fees and timing differences. Treat the output as a planning scenario rather than a forecast.

What a step-up SIP changes

A step-up SIP increases the regular contribution over time instead of keeping it constant. The increase can be expressed as a percentage or another schedule depending on the product and calculator. Mathematically, raising contributions can have two effects: you invest more principal, and the additional contributions have time to compound after they are invested.

This makes the step-up feature particularly useful when income is expected to rise. A contribution that is comfortable today may be increased after a salary raise rather than starting with an amount that strains the current budget.

How to compare a flat SIP with a step-up SIP

Keep the return assumption and investment horizon identical, then compare the total contributions and projected value. The difference is not “free” growth: part of the larger final value comes from putting more money into the investment. Separate additional contributions from investment growth when interpreting the result.

Important limitations

The assumed return is still uncertain, and a higher contribution does not remove investment risk. A step-up schedule should also be realistic relative to income and expenses. If your income is irregular, consider a contribution plan that remains sustainable during weaker months.

For a deeper comparison, use the SIP calculator alongside the step-up scenario and test more than one return assumption.
