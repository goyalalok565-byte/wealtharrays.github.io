EMI & Loan Payments: How Amortization Really Works

A fixed-payment loan can feel simple because the monthly payment stays stable. Behind that payment is an amortization schedule that gradually shifts money from interest toward principal.

The EMI formula

For a fixed-rate loan, EMI = [P × r × (1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]. P is the original principal, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly payments.

Why early payments contain more interest

Interest for a period is based on the outstanding balance. At the beginning, the balance is largest, so the interest portion is larger. After principal has been paid down, the same rate produces less interest and more of the payment can reduce principal.

Extra payments

Additional principal payments can reduce the balance earlier and therefore reduce future interest. The exact benefit depends on the loan contract, timing, fees and whether the lender shortens the term or lowers the payment.

What this calculator assumes

The standard calculator assumes a fixed rate and regular monthly payments. Real mortgages and loans can include fees, insurance, taxes, rate resets or other terms that require a more detailed schedule.
