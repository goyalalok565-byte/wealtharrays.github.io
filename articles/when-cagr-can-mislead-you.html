When CAGR Can Mislead You

CAGR is useful for comparing long-term growth, but it can hide the path an investment took to get there.

What CAGR does well

CAGR converts a beginning value and an ending value into an equivalent constant annual growth rate. That makes investments held for different lengths of time easier to compare.

What it hides

It does not show volatility. An investment that fell sharply and later recovered can have the same CAGR as one that grew smoothly.

It is not your personal return in every situation

If you added or withdrew money during the period, a simple CAGR based only on the start and end value may not represent your actual experience.

Use it with context

Check the holding period, the size of gains and losses along the way, fees, taxes and the assumptions behind the comparison.

Bottom line

CAGR is a clean summary, not a complete story. Use it to compare growth rates, then investigate what happened underneath the number.
