When CAGR Can Mislead You

CAGR, or compound annual growth rate, converts a starting value, ending value and holding period into one annualized growth rate. It is useful, but it deliberately smooths the path between the two values.

Written and maintained by Wealth Arrays. Last reviewed: September 14, 2026.

The formula

CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Starting value)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. If ₹10,000 becomes ₹12,100 over two years, the CAGR is 10%. It does not mean the investment earned exactly 10% in each year.

What CAGR is good at

CAGR gives a clean annualized comparison for a simple start-to-finish investment. It is often easier to compare than a raw total gain when holding periods differ.

What CAGR hides

CAGR does not show volatility, drawdowns or the order of returns. Two investments can have the same start value, end value and CAGR while taking very different paths.

Cash flows change the question

If money was added or withdrawn during the period, a simple CAGR can fail to describe the investor's actual experience. Cash-flow-aware measures such as IRR or XIRR answer a different question and may be more appropriate.

Historical versus assumed CAGR

A historical CAGR describes what happened between two known values. A projected CAGR is a mathematical assumption. Neither means the same rate is guaranteed to continue.

Use context

  • Check the holding period.
  • Check whether there were contributions or withdrawals.
  • Consider fees and taxes.
  • Look at volatility and drawdowns.
  • Identify whether the rate is historical, assumed or contractually specified.
