ROI vs Annualized Return: When Each Number Helps

Return percentages are easy to quote and surprisingly easy to misuse. Total ROI answers one question; annualized return helps compare results across different holding periods.

Total ROI

Total ROI = (Final value − Cost) / Cost × 100. If an investment rises from 10,000 to 14,500, the total gain is 4,500 and total ROI is 45% before considering fees, taxes or cash flows.

Annualized return

For a simple one-time investment with no intermediate cash flows, annualized return can be expressed as (Final value / Cost)^(1/years) − 1. This is often called CAGR when applied to a suitable investment series.

Why the distinction matters

A 40% total return over one year is very different from a 40% total return over ten years. Annualization puts the result on a common yearly scale, although it still does not capture volatility or the path taken to reach the final value.

Watch the cash flows

Regular contributions and withdrawals can make a basic ROI formula misleading. In those situations, a money-weighted or time-weighted return method may be more appropriate depending on the question being asked.
