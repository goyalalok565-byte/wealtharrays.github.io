How to Compare SIP Scenarios Properly

A SIP projection becomes useful when you compare assumptions rather than staring at one large future-value number. The three biggest planning variables are contribution, time and assumed return.

Written and maintained by Wealth Arrays. Last reviewed: September 14, 2026.

Keep the comparison fair

When comparing two scenarios, change one major variable at a time. If one scenario has a larger contribution, a longer period and a higher return, you cannot tell which change caused the difference. A clean comparison isolates the trade-off.

Compare contribution first

Contribution is the part of the plan you control most directly. Compare a sustainable monthly amount with a higher amount and see how much the target changes. If the higher contribution would damage emergency savings or essential cash flow, the mathematical improvement may not be a practical improvement.

Then compare time

Starting earlier can give contributions more periods to compound. Compare a shorter and longer horizon using the same contribution and return assumption. The difference illustrates the value of time without requiring an optimistic rate.

Use a range of returns

For uncertain investments, run a lower, middle and higher assumption. Do not choose the scenario that gives the target you want and call it a forecast. A robust plan should remain understandable even when the return assumption is reduced.

Inflation changes the meaning of the target

If the target is a future spending goal, nominal future value is only part of the picture. Inflation can reduce purchasing power. Compare the future amount with a separate inflation scenario so you understand what the target may represent in today's terms.

What a SIP calculator does not model automatically

Constant-return models usually do not reproduce market volatility, fund-specific fees, taxes, transaction costs or irregular contribution behavior unless those features are explicitly built into the tool. Treat the result as a planning model, not a performance guarantee.

A practical three-scenario table

For a useful review, record monthly contribution, years, assumed return, total contributions and projected value for each scenario. Then ask which assumption is doing the most work. That question is often more useful than the final number itself.
