How to Compare SIP Scenarios Properly

A practical way to compare monthly investments without treating one assumed return as a promise.

The mistake: comparing only the biggest final number

A SIP projection depends heavily on the return rate and the number of years. A calculator can show a large future value, but that does not make the assumption realistic.

Run three cases

Use a cautious, middle and optimistic return assumption. Keep the monthly amount and time period the same first. The range between outcomes tells you more about uncertainty than one attractive projection.

Then change one variable at a time

Increase the monthly investment while keeping the rate unchanged. Then extend the investment period. This shows whether your goal depends more on saving more, staying invested longer, or assuming higher returns.

Do not confuse nominal money with purchasing power

A future corpus can look impressive while buying less than the same number today. Compare the inflation-adjusted result before treating a target as sufficient.

Bottom line

A SIP calculator is strongest as a scenario tool. It cannot promise a market return, but it can show how your assumptions change the possible outcome.
