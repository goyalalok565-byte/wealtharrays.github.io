How to Calculate Real Return After Inflation

Nominal growth tells you how the number changed. Real return asks how purchasing power changed after inflation.

The exact formula

The inflation-adjusted real return is: (1 + nominal return) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1. This is more precise than simply subtracting inflation from the nominal return, especially when rates are large.

Example

If an investment earns 10% and inflation is 6%, the exact real return is about 3.77%, not exactly 4%. The difference comes from compounding the inflation adjustment rather than treating the two rates as simple additions and subtractions.

Why the distinction matters

A future balance can look much larger in currency terms while buying less than the same percentage increase suggests. Comparing nominal future value with its inflation-adjusted purchasing power creates a more realistic planning picture.
