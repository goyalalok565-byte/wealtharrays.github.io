SIP Calculator Guide: How Monthly Investing Compounds Over Time

A SIP is a recurring investment: the same amount is added at regular intervals. The calculator estimates what those contributions could become if a chosen annual return continued throughout the period.

The basic SIP formula

For monthly contributions, a common future-value formula is FV = P × [((1+i)^n − 1) / i] × (1+i). P is the monthly contribution, i is the monthly rate, and n is the number of monthly contributions. When the rate is zero, the result is simply the contribution multiplied by the number of months.

A worked example

Suppose you invest 200 units each month for 15 years and use a 10% annual return as a hypothetical assumption. You contribute 36,000 units over 180 months. The projected value can be substantially higher because earlier contributions have more time to compound.

What the calculator does not know

A projection is not a promise. Real investments can have uneven returns, fees, taxes, inflation and periods of loss. A constant annual rate is useful for understanding the mathematics, but it is not a forecast of market performance.

Common mistakes

Do not treat an expected return as guaranteed. Do not compare a pre-tax projection with an after-tax investment without adjusting the inputs. Also check whether a monthly, quarterly or annual contribution assumption matches the product you are actually considering.
