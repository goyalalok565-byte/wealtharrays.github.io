How to Use an Inflation Calculator

Inflation calculations help translate a price or money target across time. The key is to treat the inflation rate as an assumption rather than a guaranteed forecast.

Open the Inflation Calculator →

The core idea

If prices rise over time, the same amount of money generally buys fewer goods and services than before. A basic inflation model compounds the assumed annual rate across the number of years in the scenario.

A simple example

Suppose an item costs ₹10 lakh today and you model 6% annual inflation for 10 years. The model applies the assumed rate repeatedly to estimate a future price. The result is a scenario, not a promise that the item will actually cost that amount.

Future price versus purchasing power

There are two useful questions. First: “What might today's price become under this inflation assumption?” Second: “What might a future amount be worth in today's purchasing-power terms?” They are related but answer different planning questions.

Why the rate matters so much

Small changes in the assumed inflation rate can create large differences over long periods because the effect compounds. For that reason, it is often better to run a range of plausible assumptions rather than rely on one precise-looking percentage.

Where inflation estimates can mislead

  1. Using one rate for everything. Housing, education, healthcare and everyday goods can change at different rates.
  2. Confusing nominal and real values. A future rupee amount is not automatically equivalent to the same number today.
  3. Ignoring the time horizon. Long horizons amplify assumption differences.
  4. Treating a scenario as a forecast. Calculator output depends entirely on the selected assumptions.

Connect inflation to planning

Inflation is especially important when setting a long-term goal. Pair the Inflation Calculator with the Retirement Calculator to stress-test spending targets, or compare an inflation-adjusted goal with the Compound Interest Calculator to see how growth assumptions interact with purchasing-power assumptions.

Limitations

This is a mathematical scenario model, not an economic forecast. Actual inflation varies over time and across goods and services. Use multiple assumptions and revisit long-term plans as circumstances change.

Bottom line

An inflation calculator is most useful when it turns an abstract percentage into a concrete planning question: what could this price or target look like later, and how sensitive is the answer to the assumption?
