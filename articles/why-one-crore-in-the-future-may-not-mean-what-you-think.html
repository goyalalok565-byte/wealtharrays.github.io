FINANCIAL PLANNING GUIDE

Why ₹1 Crore in the Future May Not Mean What You Think

Inflation changes purchasing power, so a large future target should be translated into what it may actually buy.

Nominal money and purchasing power are different

₹1 crore is a number of rupees. What matters for planning is what those rupees can buy at the time you need them.

Inflation compounds too

When prices rise repeatedly over many years, the gap between today's purchasing power and a future rupee amount can become substantial.

Translate goals into today's money

Start with the lifestyle or expense you want to fund today. Then model how that cost could change under different inflation assumptions.

Avoid pretending to know the exact future

Inflation rates can change. Use multiple scenarios rather than treating one rate as a forecast.

Bottom line

A future target should always be read alongside its estimated purchasing power. The headline number alone is not enough.
