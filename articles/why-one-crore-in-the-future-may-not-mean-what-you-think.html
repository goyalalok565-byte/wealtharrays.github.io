PLANNING GUIDE

Why ₹1 Crore in the Future May Not Mean What You Think

A future target of ₹1 crore sounds precise, but the number alone does not tell you what that money will buy. Inflation changes purchasing power over time, so a long-term goal should be tested in both nominal and real terms.

Written and maintained by Wealth Arrays. Last reviewed: September 14, 2026.

Nominal money versus real purchasing power

Nominal value is the number printed on the account. Real value asks what that amount can buy relative to today's prices. If prices rise over time, the same nominal amount buys fewer goods and services.

A simple inflation example

Suppose inflation averaged 6% for 20 years. Prices would be roughly 3.21 times higher under that constant mathematical assumption. A future ₹1 crore would therefore have purchasing power comparable to roughly ₹31 lakh in today's-price terms. This is an illustration, not a forecast of future inflation.

Why target inflation matters

If you are planning a retirement or education goal, the relevant question is not just “How large can the account become?” It is “How large does the account need to be to pay for the future expense?” Estimating the future cost first can produce a more useful target.

Do not overreact to the calculation

Inflation varies. A single constant rate is a planning assumption. Use a range of inflation scenarios and revisit the target as real-world prices change. The purpose of the model is to show sensitivity, not to create false certainty.

Connect inflation to saving

Once the future spending target is estimated, use a calculator to compare the monthly contribution or time horizon needed under different return assumptions. That creates a more complete plan than choosing a round target such as ₹1 crore without considering the expense it is meant to fund.

Questions to ask

  • What expense is the target meant to cover?
  • What inflation assumption is being used?
  • What happens if inflation is higher?
  • Is the return assumption nominal or real?
  • Are taxes and fees included?
