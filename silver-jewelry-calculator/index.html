What this calculator does

Estimate silver jewellery value with purity, making charges and a tax assumption. It keeps the assumptions visible so you can change one input at a time and understand what drives the result.

How the calculation works

Silver value = rate per gram × weight × purity; the estimate then adds making charges and tax.

Inputs

The calculator uses silver rate, weight, purity, making charge and tax. Change the assumptions and click Calculate result to compare scenarios.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs deterministic browser-side calculations. It does not automatically know current market rates, lender policy, tax law, fees or personal eligibility. Verify current figures with the relevant official source or provider before making a material decision.

Frequently asked questions

Does this use a live silver price?

No. Enter a current rate from a reputable source or jeweller.

Why does purity matter?

Lower purity means less fine silver for the same gross weight.

Is the shop price exact?

Not necessarily; labour, wastage, margins and taxes can change it.

Silver jewellery cost: metal value is only one component

A silver-jewellery estimate can combine net silver weight, the assumed silver price per gram, purity, making charges and applicable taxes. The result depends on the unit and purity basis used in the price quote. A retail bill may include design or labour charges that are not recovered on resale. Confirm whether the jeweller's quoted rate is per gram of pure silver or for a particular purity and whether the displayed price already includes tax.

Check purity, net weight and units

Confirm whether the weight includes stones, enamel, thread or other non-silver components. Hallmarking and purity declarations can help identify the metal standard, but the rate applied to the bill still needs to be understood. Keep the price unit consistent—grams, kilograms, or another unit—and convert before multiplying. Do not compare two shops using gross weight if one excludes non-metal parts and the other does not.

Purchase price and resale value differ

Retail making charges, taxes and design premiums may not be recovered when selling or exchanging jewellery. Buy-back deductions, testing and purity checks may also affect proceeds. This calculator estimates a scenario from inputs; it does not verify the live market price, authenticity, hallmark or a jeweller's buy-back policy. Ask for a detailed invoice before purchase.

Use an itemised invoice to validate the estimate

Ask the seller to show the net silver weight, declared purity, rate per gram, making charge, tax and any stone or accessory charge separately. Confirm whether the quoted metal rate already reflects the item's purity; applying a purity adjustment twice will understate the value. If a piece contains stones or other materials, the gross scale weight may not equal the silver weight used for pricing. Compare quotes only when both sellers use the same purity basis and include the same charge categories. Retail making charges may not be recovered on resale, so purchase cost and resale value should be considered separately. This tool cannot authenticate silver or verify a live market quote.

Compare purchases using the same pricing method

For a fair comparison, request the same details from each jeweller: purity, net metal weight, per-gram rate, making charge, tax and any discount. Check whether the discount applies to metal value, labour or the whole invoice. If the jewellery includes stones or decorative parts, ask how their weight and cost are treated. Keep the invoice because resale and exchange policies may use different purity tests and deductions. A low headline price can be offset by higher labour charges or a lower buy-back value. The estimate should be used to question and reconcile an itemised quote, not as proof that a product is genuine or fairly priced.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For illustration, 30 grams of jewellery at a quoted ₹90 per gram has a base metal-value estimate of ₹2,700 before making charges and taxes. If the item is not 100% pure or the quoted rate uses a different purity basis, the simple multiplication can misstate the metal component. Add the jeweller's written making-charge calculation and current tax amount to estimate the invoice total.
