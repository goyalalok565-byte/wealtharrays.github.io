What this calculator does

Work backward from a target amount, current savings, expected return and time horizon. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

First grow the current corpus at the assumed monthly rate. The remaining target is divided by the future-value factor for equal monthly contributions. This model assumes contributions at the start of each month and a constant return, which real investments will not deliver exactly.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Target corpus; Current investment corpus; Assumed annual return; Time to goal; Required monthly SIP; Future value of current corpus; Target gap after current corpus growth. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Target corpus: 5000000; Current investment corpus: 500000; Assumed annual return: 10; Time to goal: 15. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does this guarantee I will reach the target?

No. The required SIP is a mathematical estimate based on a constant assumed return. Actual returns vary and the target may not be reached.

What if my current savings already grow beyond the target?

The calculator returns zero as the required monthly SIP if the assumed growth of your current corpus already meets or exceeds the target.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

Calculate a monthly SIP for a target corpus

A goal-based SIP calculator works backward from a target amount, current investments, time horizon and assumed return to estimate the monthly contribution needed. The output is sensitive to the target date and return assumption. If current savings are already projected to meet the target under the chosen assumption, the required SIP may be zero; that does not guarantee the goal will be reached in real markets. Consider inflation if the target is a future expense.

Set the target in future rupees

A target for education, a home deposit or retirement may cost more in the future because prices rise. If you enter today's cost as the target without adjusting for inflation, the required SIP can be understated. Estimate the future expense first, then calculate the contribution. Separate a firm deadline from a flexible goal because a shorter horizon generally requires larger contributions.

Review the plan over time

Investment values and household savings capacity change. Revisit the target, current corpus and remaining time periodically; do not keep using the original time horizon after a missed contribution or market decline. A required SIP is a scenario estimate, not personalised investment advice or a guarantee. Choose a contribution that leaves room for essential spending and emergency reserves.

Worked example with Indian rupees

For a target of ₹50,00,000 in 15 years, with ₹5,00,000 already invested and a hypothetical 10% nominal annual return compounded monthly, the existing investment grows to about ₹22.27 lakh in the model. The additional monthly end-of-month SIP needed is approximately ₹6,691 to bridge the remaining target under these assumptions. Run lower-return scenarios such as 7% and include inflation if the target represents a future expense; the projection is not guaranteed.
