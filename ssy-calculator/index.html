What this calculator does

Estimate the maturity value of a Sukanya Samriddhi account from the yearly deposit, deposit period and the interest rate in force. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

This estimate adds the yearly deposit at the start of each year for the chosen number of deposit years (at most 15) and then applies the annual interest rate to the running balance, compounding once a year until the account reaches 21 years from opening. Real accounts credit interest on the lowest monthly balance and the rate is reset every quarter, so the actual maturity value can differ from this simplified projection.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Yearly deposit; Years of deposits; Assumed annual interest rate; Total deposited; Interest earned; Estimated maturity value at 21 years; Balance when deposits end. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Yearly deposit: 100000; Years of deposits: 15; Assumed annual interest rate: 8.2. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 8.2% rate fixed for the whole period?

No. The government notifies the rate for small-savings schemes every quarter, so it can rise or fall. The rate field is an assumption; update it to the latest notified figure.

How long do deposits continue?

Deposits are made for 15 years from opening, while the account itself matures 21 years after opening. In the last six years the balance keeps earning interest without new deposits.

What are the deposit limits?

The scheme allows a minimum of ₹250 and a maximum of ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year. Confirm current limits, eligibility and tax treatment with your bank or post office.

What a Sukanya Samriddhi projection can and cannot show

The Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana is a government-backed small savings scheme for a girl child, and its maturity value depends on three things you control and one you do not. You control the yearly deposit and the number of deposit years. You do not control the interest rate, which is notified every quarter. That is why this calculator treats the rate as an editable assumption. Enter the latest notified rate and then try a lower figure to see how sensitive the maturity value is. The projection compounds once a year and assumes the deposit is made at the start of each year, which is the most favourable timing, so a real account with later deposits may end slightly lower.

The last six years do a lot of the work

Deposits stop after 15 years but the account keeps earning until it reaches 21 years. In the example, the balance grows from about ₹29.8 lakh at the end of year 15 to about ₹47.9 lakh at year 21 without any new deposit. Shortening the number of deposit years from 15 to 10 cuts the estimate to about ₹37.7 lakh, which shows how much the later contributions and the long compounding period add together.

Check the scheme rules before you rely on the figure

Deposit limits, eligibility, the age of the girl child, withdrawal conditions and tax treatment are set by the government and can be revised. This tool does not decide eligibility or model partial withdrawals. Use the official notification, your bank or your post office for the current rate and rules, and use this page to understand how the numbers behave when the rate or the deposit changes.

Worked example with Indian rupees

A yearly deposit of ₹1,00,000 for 15 years at 8.2% totals ₹15,00,000 in deposits. Left to grow until year 21, the balance reaches an estimated ₹47,88,079, of which about ₹32,88,079 is interest. The balance at the end of the deposit period, in year 15, is about ₹29,83,993. At the maximum yearly deposit of ₹1,50,000 the estimated maturity value rises to about ₹71,82,119, and at a lower 7.2% rate the ₹1,00,000 plan reaches about ₹41,51,802.
