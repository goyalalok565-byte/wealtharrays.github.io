What this calculator does

The retirement calculator helps you explore whether a target retirement balance could be reached from your current savings, regular contributions, expected return and time horizon. It is a scenario-planning tool rather than a promise about how much money you will need or how markets will perform.

Retirement planning is especially sensitive to assumptions. Inflation, spending changes, taxes, investment fees, retirement age and the length of retirement can all materially change the result.

How the projection works

The model combines the future value of existing savings with the future value of recurring contributions under the selected return assumption. Contributions and compounding are applied according to the calculator's stated timing convention. The result is then compared with the target or withdrawal assumptions represented by the tool.

The important point is that a mathematical projection can be internally consistent while still being uncertain in real life. A constant annual return is a simplifying assumption, not a forecast of yearly market performance.

Worked scenario

Suppose you start with $25,000, add $500 each month and have 25 years until a target retirement date. Test several annual return assumptions instead of selecting only a high historical average. Then change the monthly contribution to see whether increasing savings has a larger effect than changing the assumed return.

Inflation and retirement spending

A retirement target stated in today's currency may not represent the same purchasing power decades from now. Inflation can make future living costs higher even when the nominal investment balance also grows. A sound planning exercise therefore considers both the future account value and the future spending requirement.

Withdrawal planning is also uncertain. Retirement length, healthcare costs, taxes, market sequence and changing spending patterns can affect whether a portfolio lasts.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays calculates scenarios from your inputs in the browser. It does not know your pension, government benefits, investment allocation, tax jurisdiction or actual spending. The result is educational and should be checked against current product rules and, when appropriate, professional advice.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the 4% figure come from?

A 4% withdrawal assumption is sometimes used as a planning example, but it is not a universal rule or guarantee. The appropriate withdrawal approach depends on circumstances and market conditions.

Does the calculator account for inflation?

Use the calculator's inflation-related input when available. Otherwise, treat the displayed balance as nominal and separately consider how prices may change over the planning period.

Is this a pension projection?

No. It is a generic mathematical scenario. Pension plans and government benefits have their own eligibility rules, formulas and assumptions.

Why compare multiple scenarios?

Because retirement outcomes can be highly sensitive to contribution amounts, return assumptions, inflation and time. A range of scenarios is more informative than one precise-looking number.
