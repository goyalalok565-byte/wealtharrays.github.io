What this calculator does

Estimate a future EPF balance using salary, contribution percentages, an assumed interest rate and years remaining. Change the assumptions to compare outcomes. The result is an estimate based on the inputs you provide, not a promise or personalised financial recommendation.

How the calculation works

Each month, this estimate adds the employee contribution plus the employer EPF contribution you enter, then applies the monthly equivalent of the assumed annual rate. Basic pay is increased once per year by the assumed salary growth rate. Employer EPS allocation, statutory ceilings, withdrawals and tax rules are not modelled.

How to use this calculator

For this calculator, check these inputs and units: Monthly basic pay; Employee contribution; Employer EPF contribution; Annual basic-pay growth; Assumed annual EPF interest; Years until withdrawal; Projected EPF balance; Total contributions; Estimated interest. Keep monthly and annual figures separate, and change one input at a time when comparing scenarios.

Read the result in context

The output is the mathematical result of the assumptions entered, not a forecast of what a bank, employer, tax authority or investment provider will necessarily apply. Separate amounts you contribute or pay from interest, growth, tax or other calculated components where the tool provides them. A larger final amount is not always a better outcome: total interest, liquidity, affordability, risk and the time available can matter just as much as the headline number. Keep a note of your inputs when comparing two scenarios.

Compare more than one scenario

Run a baseline estimate first. Then create a conservative case and a second case with one important assumption changed, such as the rate, period, payment or contribution. Comparing scenarios is more informative than selecting a single result because it shows how sensitive the outcome is to the inputs. For long periods, even a small change in an assumed rate can have a large effect. For short periods, fees, timing and fixed charges can be relatively more important than a percentage-rate difference.

What this estimate does not include

This calculator does not automatically know current product terms, policy changes, eligibility conditions, fees, taxes, penalties, contribution ceilings or personal circumstances. Those items can change the real amount you receive or pay. For tax-related estimates, verify the applicable tax year, regime and official rules. For deposits, loans and retirement products, compare the result with the provider current documentation. If the result will affect a major financial decision, confirm the assumptions with a qualified professional or the relevant official source.

Example inputs

One illustrative set of inputs is: Monthly basic pay: 30000; Employee contribution: 12; Employer EPF contribution: 3.67; Annual basic-pay growth: 5; Assumed annual EPF interest: 8.25; Years until withdrawal: 25. Use your own figures for a more relevant estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Is this an official EPF statement?

No. It is a projection based on the rates and contributions you enter, not a statement from EPFO.

Does it model EPS and contribution ceilings?

No. Enter only the employer contribution you expect to reach EPF. EPS allocation, wage ceilings, withdrawals and tax treatment need separate checking.

Important assumptions and limitations

This tool models the formula and assumptions described above. Actual outcomes can differ because of fees, taxes, eligibility rules, contribution timing, rate changes and individual circumstances. Check current official guidance or your provider's terms before making a financial decision.

EPF projection: employee and employer contributions

An Employees' Provident Fund projection depends on basic pay, contribution percentages, salary growth, interest assumptions and years remaining. Employer contributions are not necessarily credited entirely to EPF: part may be allocated to EPS subject to applicable rules and wage ceilings. This model uses the employer EPF percentage you enter and does not reproduce every EPFO rule, withdrawal, transfer or tax situation. Compare its estimate with your payslip and EPFO passbook.

Employee share and employer share are different

The employee contribution is commonly calculated as a percentage of eligible wages. The employer's total statutory contribution can be split between EPF and EPS, and the applicable wage ceiling or employee category may affect the calculation. Enter only the employer share intended for EPF if the model asks for that field; do not treat the full employer contribution as EPF automatically.

Use the projection as a reconciliation check

Compare the inputs with monthly payslips and the EPFO passbook, and check whether contribution wages, salary growth and credited interest match your employment terms. Withdrawals, transfers, service breaks, tax treatment and changes in rules are not automatically modelled. For an official balance or claim decision, rely on EPFO records and current official guidance.

Worked example with Indian rupees

With monthly basic pay of ₹30,000, a 12% employee share is ₹3,600 per month. If the entered employer EPF share is 3.67%, that adds ₹1,101 per month to the modelled EPF contribution, or ₹4,701 combined before interest. The employer's actual allocation can differ because EPS and statutory rules apply. Over a long horizon, salary increases and the credited rate can materially change the projected balance.
