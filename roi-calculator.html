What this calculator does

The ROI calculator measures the percentage gain or loss relative to an original amount. It is useful for quickly describing how much an amount changed, but it should not be confused with an annualized return when different investments are held for different lengths of time.

The calculator is deliberately simple. Its value is in making the arithmetic transparent and helping you compare scenarios without implying that a historical or assumed percentage is a forecast.

How ROI is calculated

The basic formula is ROI = (Ending value − Starting value) ÷ Starting value × 100. A positive result means the ending value is above the starting amount under the selected inputs. A negative result means the ending value is below it.

ROI does not automatically account for the passage of time. A 20% gain over one year and a 20% gain over ten years have the same simple ROI but very different annualized growth characteristics. Use CAGR when you need a normalized annual rate for a multi-year start and end value.

Worked example

If an amount rises from $10,000 to $12,000, the simple ROI is 20%. If the same change takes several years, the 20% figure still describes the total change but does not tell you the equivalent yearly growth rate. Enter the same values into the calculator and then compare them with the CAGR calculator to see the difference.

What ROI leaves out

Real investment analysis can require fees, taxes, dividends, cash flows, deposits, withdrawals and the timing of each cash flow. A simple ROI calculation can therefore be useful as a first description without being a complete performance measure.

When comparing real products, use the provider's statements and transaction history. If cash flows happen at different times, a time-weighted or money-weighted performance method may be more appropriate.

Limitations and methodology

Wealth Arrays performs the arithmetic in your browser and does not import investment records. The displayed result reflects only the values you enter. It should be treated as an educational calculation, not a recommendation about an investment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ROI and CAGR?

ROI describes total percentage change, while CAGR converts a start-to-end change over multiple years into an equivalent annual growth rate.

Does ROI include fees and taxes?

Not unless they are included in the values you enter. Product-specific costs and taxes should be accounted for separately when analyzing a real investment.

What does a negative ROI mean?

It means the ending value is lower than the starting value under the selected inputs. The percentage tells you the size of the change relative to the original amount.

Can ROI compare investments held for different periods?

It can describe each total change, but it is not a time-normalized comparison. CAGR or a cash-flow-aware return measure may provide more context.

When a simple ROI is not enough

If you add money at different dates, receive dividends or withdraw part of an investment, a simple start-to-end ROI can hide the effect of those cash flows. Use the calculator as a first comparison, then use transaction-level records and an appropriate return methodology when the timing of cash flows matters.
